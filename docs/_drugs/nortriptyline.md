{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Nortriptyline is a tricyclic antidepressant, listed in the knowledge base for anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder and dysthymic disorder.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)**,
with **no registered clinical trials** but **about 11 relevant publications** (including one randomized controlled trial and one Cochrane review) currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacology of the drug class, nortriptyline is a tricyclic antidepressant that mainly inhibits norepinephrine reuptake, with weaker serotonin reuptake inhibition. Pharmacology data compiled for this report show activity at the norepinephrine transporter (NET) and the serotonin transporter (SERT), as well as at the α1A-adrenoceptor and several inwardly rectifying potassium channels (Kir3.2, Kir3.4, Kir4.1).

Enhancing noradrenergic signalling in prefrontal circuits is the same pathway targeted by atomoxetine and other non-stimulant ADHD treatments. This offers a plausible mechanistic link between a drug used for mood and anxiety conditions and ADHD. A PET study in patients with depression also confirmed that nortriptyline occupies NET in the human brain.

The very high TxGNN score is a computational prediction and does not replace clinical evidence. A second predicted term, ADHD inattentive type (score 0.9933), is a subtype of the same condition. It has no specific trials or literature and largely mirrors the parent term, so it is not evaluated separately here.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (knowledge graph, not label text)**: Cardiac conditions (long QT syndrome, torsades de pointes, myocardial infarction, heart conduction disease, sudden cardiac arrest), liver failure, kidney failure, epilepsy, manic bipolar disorder, glaucoma, benign prostatic hyperplasia, substance abuse/dependence, stroke and cerebrovascular disorders, hyperthyroidism, and hemoglobinopathy.
- **Cardiovascular risk**: The literature notes a narrow therapeutic index and potential cardiovascular toxicity for tricyclics in ADHD, and ECG effects in children have been studied retrospectively.
- **Pharmacological interactions**: Activity at NET, SERT, the α1A-adrenoceptor and Kir channels was identified. No specific drug-drug interaction levels were reported.

Please refer to the package insert for full safety information, as label warnings and contraindications are not available in the data compiled for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Published evidence (one pediatric RCT, a Cochrane review and several observational studies) supports a plausible role in ADHD, but no registered clinical trials exist, and tricyclic cardiovascular risk limits use. Label safety data are missing.
