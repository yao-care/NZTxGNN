## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Ipilimumab blocks CTLA-4 on T cells, which enhances T-cell priming and anti-tumour immunity. This is the basis of its use in melanoma. The data compiled for this report contain no separate detailed mechanism-of-action record.

On review, there is no credible mechanistic link to choroideremia. This condition is a monogenic, X-linked inherited retinal degeneration. Loss of function of the *CHM* gene (REP1 protein) causes progressive chorioretinal atrophy. The disease is not driven by CTLA-4-mediated T-cell checkpoint inhibition, so ipilimumab has no plausible therapeutic target here.

Immune checkpoint blockade could also add risk. Ocular immune-related adverse events such as uveitis could harm a retina that is already vulnerable. The high TxGNN score most likely reflects a TxGNN model (deep learning) artifact, because no clinical or literature evidence supports it.

## Safety Considerations

- **Ocular risk**: Checkpoint blockade may cause immune-related ocular events such as uveitis. This is a particular concern in an already degenerating retina.
- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen returned 100 interactions in total. The main ones among those reviewed are:
  - **Major**: anticoagulants, including apixaban, betrixaban, edoxaban, dalteparin, danaparoid, argatroban, bivalirudin and desirudin.
  - **Moderate**: corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, prednisone, prednisolone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, budesonide), metronidazole, tinidazole, naltrexone, and the statins rosuvastatin and simvastatin.

Please refer to the package insert for other safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps
