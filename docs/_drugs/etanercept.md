## Etanercept: Hypermobility of coccyx
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Etanercept is a TNF-blocking fusion protein originally used for inflammatory arthritis, including rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis and juvenile idiopathic arthritis.
Some of that literature actually reports etanercept-associated vasculitis, so the evidence is weak and partly cautionary.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, etanercept is a soluble TNF receptor fusion protein that neutralises TNF-alpha. Its efficacy in rheumatoid arthritis is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to rheumatoid vasculitis.

Rheumatoid vasculitis is a severe extra-articular complication of rheumatoid arthritis, an approved etanercept indication. TNF-alpha is a central driver of the underlying inflammation, so TNF blockade is biologically plausible. A systematic review of biologic therapy in rheumatoid vasculitis exists, but it rests on low-quality observational data.

There is also a paradoxical concern. Several publications describe vasculitis, cutaneous vasculitis and nephropathy arising during etanercept therapy. Direct efficacy evidence in rheumatoid vasculitis is lacking.

Other predictions in the list include inflammatory spondylopathy and polyarticular juvenile rheumatoid arthritis. These largely overlap with existing approved indications and are rediscoveries rather than true repurposing. Hypermobility of coccyx and Kummell disease have no supporting trials or literature, and no plausible TNF-driven mechanism.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found in the interaction screen. Major-level interactions include corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, triamcinolone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, prednisone, prednisolone), deferiprone, and radiopharmaceuticals/radioimmunotherapies (iobenguane I-131, ibritumomab tiuxetan, tositumomab). Moderate interactions include metronidazole, tinidazole, rosuvastatin and simvastatin. Minor interactions include zinc salts.
- **Class-related concerns from the literature**: Anti-TNF therapy is associated with reactivation of tuberculosis and other infections, disseminated herpes zoster, cutaneous vasculitis, lupus-like events and renal lesions. Disseminated zoster can mimic vasculitis and delay diagnosis.

Please refer to the package insert for formal warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The available literature is low-quality and includes reports of etanercept-associated vasculitis, which suggests a paradoxical risk.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Direct clinical evidence (trial or well-designed cohort) of etanercept in rheumatoid vasculitis, weighed against the vasculitis risk signal
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for formal safety screening

**Other predicted indications (for reference):**
- Inflammatory spondylopathy (L1, Proceed with Guardrails) and polyarticular juvenile rheumatoid arthritis (L1, Proceed with Guardrails) largely overlap with approved indications, so label scope should be confirmed before treating them as new uses.
- Vertebral disease (L4) is too non-specific to support a recommendation.
- Hypermobility of coccyx and Kummell disease (L5, Hold) have only model-based support.
