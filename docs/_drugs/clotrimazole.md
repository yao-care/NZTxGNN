# Clotrimazole: From Fungal Skin and Mucosal Infections to Vulvovaginitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Clotrimazole is a topical azole antifungal, originally used for tinea infections and for cutaneous, oral and vulvovaginal candidiasis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **vulvovaginitis** (the top-ranked prediction, acne, has weak supporting evidence), with **12 clinical trials** and **20 publications** compiled for this direction.
Because vulvovaginal candidiasis is already an approved use, this is largely an on-label use rather than true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, clotrimazole is an azole antifungal that inhibits ergosterol biosynthesis in fungal cells, giving a fungistatic effect against Candida and dermatophytes. Its efficacy in candidiasis is well established.

Vulvovaginal candidiasis is the main cause of vulvovaginitis, and it is already an approved indication for clotrimazole. The high TxGNN score is therefore consistent with the existing indication. Non-Candida causes such as bacterial vaginosis or trichomoniasis would not be expected to respond.

The other predictions are weaker:
- **Acne (score 99.86%, Hold, L4):** The link is indirect. Acne is driven mainly by sebum, *Cutibacterium acnes* and inflammation. The only trial tests a fixed combination (beclomethasone + gentamicin + clotrimazole), was suspended, and cannot isolate clotrimazole's contribution.
- **Postmenopausal atrophic vaginitis (score 99.46%, Hold, L5):** This condition results from estrogen deficiency and is not primarily infectious. The score probably reflects network proximity to vulvovaginal candidiasis. No trials or literature were found.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related conditions in the knowledge graph** (not label text): adrenocortical insufficiency, Addison disease, skin atrophy, telangiectasis, capillary disease, hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia, peripheral vascular disease, intermittent claudication and peripheral arterial disease.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were found. Examples of **Major** interactions: fentanyl, alfentanil, hydrocodone and amiodarone. Examples of **Moderate** interactions: abemaciclib, paclitaxel, acalabrutinib, nifedipine, amlodipine, levonorgestrel, norethisterone, estradiol, alprazolam, apixaban, aprepitant, alfuzosin and aliskiren. Examples of **Minor** interactions: dolutegravir and amphotericin B.
- **Pregnancy-related signals:** A large Japanese claims study and a placenta-model study examined first-trimester topical antifungal exposure and azole effects on placental steroidogenesis. These warrant attention in pregnant patients, who are commonly affected by vulvovaginal candidiasis.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available for this report; please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
For vulvovaginitis (essentially vulvovaginal candidiasis), the evidence is strong: completed Phase 3 trials and decades of clinical experience, with a currently marketed New Zealand product. However, this is an existing indication rather than a new one, and the label-based safety data are missing. The acne and atrophic vaginitis predictions should remain on **Hold**, as they rely on weak or absent evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Medsafe package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indication text
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A review of major drug interactions (opioids, amiodarone) and of use in pregnancy
- Confirmation that the cancelled Clomazol licences do not affect current vaginal-formulation availability, since only the Canesten 1% solution remains valid
