## Ropinirole: Attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Ropinirole is a dopamine D2/D3 agonist originally used to treat restless legs syndrome (RLS) and Parkinson disease.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)**,
with **no registered clinical trials** but **8 publications** currently supporting this direction, including one recent randomized, placebo-controlled trial of ropinirole added to methylphenidate.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, ropinirole is a dopamine agonist acting on D2 and D3 receptors. Its efficacy in RLS and Parkinson disease is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to ADHD.

ADHD involves dopaminergic dysregulation, and it frequently co-occurs with RLS, an established ropinirole indication. A shared dopaminergic and iron-metabolism pathway plausibly links the two conditions. This link is largely inferred from the RLS comorbidity. Direct efficacy of ropinirole in ADHD has not been firmly established.

Notably, a 2026 randomized trial tested ropinirole as an add-on to methylphenidate in children with ADHD. Its design is promising, but the result should be verified in the full text and replicated before drawing conclusions.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph (not label text)**: multiple system atrophy, postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome, orthostatic hypotension, drug-induced dyskinesia, liver failure, psychotic disorder, schizophreniform disorder, mental disorder, schizoaffective disorder, hypotensive disorder.
- **Pharmacological targets**: dopamine D2 receptor (DRD2) and D3 receptor (DRD3). No specific drug-drug interaction entries were identified.
- **Literature signals**: corneal edema with systemic dopaminergic agents, and an increased risk of gambling disorder in a Swedish nationwide register study of ropinirole, pramipexole and aripiprazole.

Please also refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high and a recent randomized trial exists, but there are no registered trials, the trial result is unconfirmed, and the other evidence is mostly case reports, reviews and preclinical work (L3).

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Full-text review of the 2026 RCT (PMID 42640474) to confirm efficacy, safety and effect size
- Independent replication in larger, multi-centre trials, ideally registered
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Medsafe
- Review of the regulatory pathway; the drug is marketed, and all four New Zealand licences are valid
- A safety plan covering impulse-control and gambling risk, psychiatric effects, orthostatic hypotension and paediatric use
