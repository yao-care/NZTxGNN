## One-Sentence Summary

Clozapine is an atypical antipsychotic that blocks several brain receptors, including dopamine, serotonin, muscarinic, adrenergic and histamine receptors.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **manic bipolar affective disorder**.
This direction is currently supported by **1 completed Phase 2 clinical trial** and **20 publications**, including a systematic review and meta-analysis. There are no Phase 3 trials for this prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Clozapine has a broad receptor profile: it antagonises D2/D4 dopamine, 5-HT2A serotonin, muscarinic, adrenergic and histamine receptors. This profile plausibly reduces manic symptoms and mood instability. It is also the reason clozapine is considered when standard treatments fail or are not tolerated.

Many patients with mania do not respond adequately to conventional treatment or cannot tolerate effective doses. A completed double-blind Phase 2 trial tested clozapine in treatment-resistant mania. A systematic review and meta-analysis of small comparative studies found clozapine similar to other antipsychotics in manic episodes and better in treatment-resistant bipolar disorder. The evidence is therefore strongest for treatment-resistant presentations, not for first-line use.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found in the interaction check. Those rated Major among the first 20 reviewed include **bupropion**, **morphine** and **potassium citrate**. Moderate interactions include antidiabetic drugs (metformin, pioglitazone, alogliptin, canagliflozin, acarbose, chlorpropamide), anticholinergic agents (atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium), acid-suppressing drugs (omeprazole, famotidine, cimetidine) and epinephrine.
- **Class-related risks noted in the literature**: neutropenia and agranulocytosis, myocarditis and cardiomyopathy, seizures, and metabolic effects including weight gain and metabolic syndrome. Clozapine may also induce or aggravate obsessive-compulsive symptoms.

For full warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
A completed Phase 2 double-blind trial and a systematic review and meta-analysis support clozapine in treatment-resistant mania and bipolar disorder. This supports an evidence level of L2. The trial is small, no published results are available in the data compiled for this report, and there are no Phase 3 trials. Clozapine is already marketed in New Zealand, but its serious, monitored risks call for restricted use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the Medsafe package insert for warnings and contraindications
- Confirmation of detailed mechanism-of-action data
- A monitoring plan covering neutropenia and agranulocytosis, myocarditis, seizures and metabolic risk
- Review of the major interactions, particularly bupropion, morphine and potassium citrate
- Results of the completed Phase 2 trial, or larger controlled trials in adults with treatment-resistant mania, to strengthen efficacy evidence
