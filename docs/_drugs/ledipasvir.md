## Ledipasvir: From Chronic Hepatitis C to Hepatitis B Virus Infection
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Ledipasvir is an HCV NS5A inhibitor, used in combination with sofosbuvir (Harvoni) to treat chronic hepatitis C.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **hepatitis B virus infection**,
with **5 clinical trials** and **20 publications** matched to this prediction. Only one completed Phase 2 study tests the HBV indication directly, and the wider literature mainly describes HBV reactivation risk during HCV treatment.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Ledipasvir is known to inhibit the HCV NS5A protein, and it is used with the polymerase inhibitor sofosbuvir. Its efficacy in hepatitis C is well established.

Both HCV and HBV cause chronic viral liver infection, which likely explains the model's prediction. Mechanistically, however, the link is weak. NS5A has no known HBV homolog, so any anti-HBV effect is not explained by the original mechanism. A pilot study of HCV/HBV co-infected patients observed a modest drop in HBsAg during ledipasvir/sofosbuvir treatment. That observation led to a dedicated study in HBV mono-infection, but it remains hypothesis-generating.

The other predicted indications scored similarly (HEV, HAV, HIV, Omsk haemorrhagic fever, Kyasanur forest disease and others). They have little or no direct supporting evidence, and several (HIV, HAV) are HCV studies matched through the drug name only. HBV has the strongest data of the group.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 65 interactions are recorded. The 20 listed in the compiled data are mostly Moderate, with two Minor (fidaxomicin, naloxegol). Main groups:
  - Acid-reducing agents: proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, dexlansoprazole, pantoprazole), H2 blockers (famotidine, cimetidine, nizatidine) and antacids (aluminium hydroxide, calcium carbonate, magnesium hydroxide, magnesium oxide, magnesium carbonate, magaldrate)
  - Other: eliglustat, linagliptin, loperamide, naldemedine
- **HBV reactivation**: Several cohort studies and case reports describe HBV reactivation during DAA treatment of HCV in patients with HBV co-infection or prior exposure. This is a key concern for any use in HBV-positive patients.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Only one small, completed open-label Phase 2 study (n=21) tests ledipasvir/sofosbuvir in HBV directly. There is no plausible HBV-specific target for an NS5A inhibitor, and the broader literature emphasises HBV reactivation rather than suppression. The only New Zealand licence is cancelled, and the package insert safety data are not available. These factors outweigh the high model score.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Full results of the Phase 2 HBV study (PMID 36045503/NCT03312023): size and durability of HBsAg and HBV DNA reduction
- A larger controlled trial in HBV mono-infection, with comparison against standard nucleos(t)ide therapy
- Mechanistic data explaining any anti-HBV activity, including whether it comes from ledipasvir or from sofosbuvir
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (Medsafe), plus a current New Zealand supply and licensing pathway
- An HBV reactivation monitoring and risk-mitigation plan, including HBV screening before treatment
