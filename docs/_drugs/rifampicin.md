# Rifampicin: From Pulmonary Tuberculosis to Conjunctivitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Rifampicin is a rifamycin antibiotic originally used to treat pulmonary tuberculosis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **conjunctivitis**.
No clinical trials are registered for this indication; the support is **about 19 publications**, mostly old studies, case reports and susceptibility surveys.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, rifampicin is a broad-spectrum antibiotic that inhibits bacterial RNA polymerase. Its efficacy in tuberculosis is established, and it is active against Gram-positive bacteria, Chlamydia and some other ocular pathogens.

Conjunctivitis is frequently caused by bacteria, including Staphylococcus, Streptococcus and Chlamydia trachomatis. An antibacterial agent with activity against these organisms could plausibly help, so the prediction is biologically reasonable. The supporting literature, however, is mainly microbiology and susceptibility surveys, a few small older topical studies (1970s) and case reports. No modern controlled trials exist, and well-established topical alternatives are available.

Other predictions in the list are weaker:
- **Acne (L4):** The retrieved papers mostly concern hidradenitis suppurativa (a different disease, where rifampicin plus clindamycin is used) and preclinical nanodroplet work. Evidence for acne vulgaris is indirect.
- **Rheumatoid arthritis (L3):** Small studies from 1988 to 1993 showed limited or inconsistent benefit, and modern DMARDs and biologics have superseded this approach.
- **HIV infection:** The trials are in HIV/TB co-infection, where rifampicin is the TB backbone. They do not show rifampicin treating HIV, so this is not a repurposing signal.
- **Simian immunodeficiency virus infection, feline AIDS, multiple endocrine neoplasia, a rare neurodevelopmental disorder and acute contagious conjunctivitis:** These are computational predictions only, with no supporting trials or direct evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related conditions (from knowledge-graph drug-disease links, not label text):** liver failure and liver disease, hepatitis, porphyria, gallbladder disease, blood disorders (thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, anaemia, pancytopenia, aplastic anaemia), diabetes and diabetic ketoacidosis, gout, peripheral neuropathy, substance abuse/dependence, slow acetylator status, anxiety and related mood disorders, and non-infectious meningitis.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified; the 20 reviewed are all Moderate severity. They include rabeprazole, omeprazole, doxycycline, clarithromycin, metronidazole, metformin, pioglitazone, canagliflozin, chlorpropamide, bupropion, aprepitant, morphine, corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone) and vitamin D analogues. Rifampicin is a strong enzyme inducer, which can lower the exposure of many co-medications (including oral contraceptives and antiretrovirals).
- **Ocular toxicity:** The literature includes reports of ocular toxicity due to rifampicin, which needs consideration for any ophthalmic use.

For other warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The antibacterial rationale for conjunctivitis is plausible, but evidence is limited to old small studies, case reports and susceptibility surveys, with no registered trials. Effective topical alternatives exist, and no rifampicin product is currently authorised in New Zealand.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Medsafe package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data
- Evidence of a topical or ophthalmic formulation and route compatibility, since the licensed forms are oral
- Comparative data against current standard conjunctivitis therapies
- Assessment of antimicrobial-resistance risk, given rifampicin's role in tuberculosis treatment
