## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, tobramycin is an aminoglycoside with broad Gram-negative and *Staphylococcus* activity. It is already used topically for bacterial eye infections, and its efficacy in conjunctivitis and other eye infections is established.

Exposure keratitis occurs when the cornea is not adequately protected (for example, when the eyelids cannot close). The damaged surface is prone to secondary bacterial infection. Tobramycin could therefore be mechanistically useful for preventing or treating that secondary infection. This is indirect support only. The drug would not treat the underlying exposure or dryness.

There is also a safety concern. An in vitro study found that aminoglycosides, including tobramycin, are toxic to corneal epithelial cells. This matters for a cornea that is already compromised.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but no clinical trials exist and none of the retrieved literature addresses exposure keratitis. There is also an in vitro corneal toxicity signal. In addition, all four New Zealand authorizations are valid, so the drug is currently marketed locally.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Medsafe (needed for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data
- Clinical evidence in exposure keratitis, specifically on preventing or treating secondary bacterial infection
- A corneal safety assessment, given the epithelial toxicity signal
- Confirmation of whether any ophthalmic tobramycin product can be supplied in New Zealand

### Other Predicted Indications (Brief Notes)

Other predictions from the model are weaker or equally indirect. Otitis externa (score 0.9981) is the only other one with notable supporting literature: tobramycin is active against *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and *Staphylococcus aureus*, and there are case-level reports in malignant external otitis. However, there are no trials, much of the literature is veterinary, and ototoxicity is a concern if the eardrum is perforated. It is rated L4 and classed as a Research Question. The remaining predictions have little or no usable evidence and are not supported:
- Non-human animal disease: not a human indication.
- Postinfectious vasculitis, post-infectious syndrome and Chagas cardiomyopathy: no evidence.
- Infection-related haemolytic uraemic syndrome: no evidence, and antibiotics and aminoglycosides carry risks in this condition.
- Post-bacterial disorder: two Phase 3 open-label extension trials of inhaled tobramycin in cystic fibrosis, which do not support this indication.
- Infective urethral stricture: one 1976 study in urinary tract infection, which is indirect.
- Epidemic keratoconjunctivitis: usually viral, so tobramycin does not target the cause.
