# Iron: From Iron Deficiency Anemia to Plummer-Vinson Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Iron is a mineral replacement therapy, originally used for iron deficiency anemia and related nutritional anemias.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is **vitamin B12- and folate-independent constitutional megaloblastic anemia**, but it has no supporting evidence. The best-supported candidate is **Plummer-Vinson syndrome** (a condition defined by iron deficiency anemia, dysphagia and esophageal webs), backed by **0 clinical trials** and **19 publications**, mostly reviews and case reports.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, iron is a replacement mineral. Its efficacy in iron deficiency anemia is established, and it is the standard way to correct the underlying deficiency.

**Rank 1 (megaloblastic anemia):** This prediction is not mechanistically plausible. Megaloblastic anemia arises from impaired DNA synthesis (B12/folate pathways or inherited defects), not iron deficiency. With no trials or literature, the high score likely reflects proximity to other anemia terms in the knowledge graph.

**Rank 2 (Plummer-Vinson syndrome):** This is the most credible prediction. The syndrome is defined by iron deficiency anemia, dysphagia and esophageal webs. Case reports and reviews describe dysphagia improving, and webs regressing, once iron is replaced. This is partly on-label use (treating the iron deficiency) rather than true repurposing, and benefit for the esophageal manifestations rests on observational and case-level sources only.

**Other predictions:** Non-syndromic esophageal malformation (structural defect), biotin metabolic disease (treated with biotin), vitamin deficiency disorder (iron does not treat vitamin deficiencies) and esophageal disease (too broad and indirect) lack a plausible mechanism or direct evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-level cautions (knowledge graph):** Iron is flagged as contraindicated or to be avoided in peptic ulcer disease, hereditary hemochromatosis, hemosiderosis, iron metabolism disease, Wilson disease, ulcerative colitis, inflammatory bowel disease, hemolytic anemia and gastroenteritis. These come from the knowledge graph rather than label text.
- **Drug Interactions:** 79 interactions were identified.
  - Major: dolutegravir, bictegravir.
  - Moderate (examples): doxycycline, tetracycline, ciprofloxacin, delafloxacin, alendronic acid, risedronic acid, ibandronate, omeprazole, rabeprazole, levodopa, cefdinir, chloramphenicol, deferiprone, magnesium oxide, sodium citrate, baloxavir marboxil, pancrelipase.
  - Minor: cimetidine.
- **Related literature note:** Intravenous iron formulations can cause hypophosphatemia (PMID 34534708).

Please refer to the package insert for label warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (for the rank 1 prediction and the other predictions). Plummer-Vinson syndrome is a research question rather than a Go.

**Rationale:**
- The top-scoring prediction has no trials, no literature and no plausible mechanism. Plummer-Vinson syndrome is supported only by reviews, case reports and one retrospective study, and iron there treats the underlying iron deficiency, which is essentially the existing indication. The only licence in New Zealand is cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Medsafe.
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank).
- Prospective or controlled data on esophageal web and dysphagia outcomes with iron in Plummer-Vinson syndrome.
- Clarification of the current regulatory position, since the sole licence is cancelled, and of which iron formulation and route would be relevant.
- Long-term endoscopic surveillance planning, given the associated esophageal cancer risk.
