The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **Liposarcoma**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Sirolimus is generally known to inhibit mTORC1, a kinase that controls cell growth and proliferation.

Activation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway has been reported in soft tissue sarcomas, including liposarcoma. Blocking mTOR is therefore a plausible way to slow tumour growth. This is the main biological link behind the prediction.

The link is still a hypothesis. The only trial tested sirolimus together with cyclophosphamide, so sirolimus's own contribution cannot be separated out. The supporting literature is mostly reviews of targeted therapy in sarcoma and papers on cancer risk after transplantation, which are indirect evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence level is L3. The only directly relevant study is one completed, single-arm Phase 2 trial of sirolimus plus cyclophosphamide, with no randomised control and no results available. The mTOR rationale is reasonable, but it cannot yet be attributed to sirolimus alone.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Published results from NCT02821507, especially the liposarcoma-specific response data
- Evidence that separates sirolimus's contribution from cyclophosphamide, ideally a randomised or sirolimus-alone study
- Mechanism of action data for the drug
- A review of the New Zealand package insert for warnings and contraindications, and a drug interaction plan covering the Major interactions above
