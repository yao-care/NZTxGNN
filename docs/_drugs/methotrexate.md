# Methotrexate: From Acute Lymphoblastic Leukaemia and Lymphoma to Primary Pulmonary Lymphoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Methotrexate is an antifolate drug, originally used for acute lymphoblastic leukaemia, non-Hodgkin lymphoma, mycosis fungoides, rheumatoid arthritis and psoriasis.
The TxGNN model ranks **primary pulmonary lymphoma** as its second-highest prediction (the top-ranked prediction, pulmonary blastoma, has no supporting data at all). It has **no registered clinical trials** and only **indirect literature** (mainly case reports and CNS lymphoma studies) behind it.
Of the predictions with some supporting data, Hodgkin lymphoma has the most trials (44 linked, mostly broad lymphoma or transplant studies), but none are Hodgkin-specific methotrexate efficacy trials.

> Note: The top-ranked prediction (pulmonary blastoma, score 99.45%) has no trials or literature. This report features primary pulmonary lymphoma (rank 2), which has the most relevant supporting literature at the top of the ranking, and summarises the other predictions at the end.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general knowledge, methotrexate is an antifolate that inhibits dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR), blocking DNA synthesis in rapidly dividing cells. Its efficacy in lymphoma and leukaemia is well established.

Primary pulmonary lymphoma is a form of non-Hodgkin lymphoma arising in the lung. Because non-Hodgkin lymphoma is already among methotrexate's original indications, this prediction is arguably an extension of an existing use rather than true repurposing. High-dose methotrexate is a backbone of regimens for CNS and other extranodal lymphomas, which gives indirect support.

However, the literature specific to the lung consists of case reports and general reviews. No pulmonary-specific methotrexate trial was found.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related conditions (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: liver failure and liver disease, kidney disease, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, anaemia (including macrocytic), interstitial lung disease, pulmonary fibrosis, peptic and gastric ulcer, ulcerative colitis and inflammatory bowel disease, stomatitis, gallbladder disease, immune system disease, substance abuse/dependence.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found in total. Major interactions include rabeprazole, amoxicillin and acetylsalicylic acid. Moderate ones include doxycycline, tetracycline, clarithromycin, corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, triamcinolone), mesalazine, balsalazide, amphotericin B and bupropion.
- **Lung-specific concern**: Methotrexate can itself cause pulmonary toxicity and, in patients with rheumatoid arthritis, methotrexate-associated lymphoproliferative disorders, which is relevant for a lung-targeted use.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Primary pulmonary lymphoma is supported only by indirect evidence (CNS lymphoma regimens, case reports) and has no registered trials. It also largely overlaps with methotrexate's existing non-Hodgkin lymphoma use. Methotrexate currently has no valid New Zealand licence, which adds a regulatory barrier.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (MOA) and the package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Pulmonary-specific efficacy data, ideally prospective, given the risk of methotrexate lung toxicity
- Confirmation of regulatory status and product availability in New Zealand
- If a different candidate is preferred, Hodgkin lymphoma (VBM regimen) and rhabdomyosarcoma are the best-supported alternatives and merit a focused literature review
