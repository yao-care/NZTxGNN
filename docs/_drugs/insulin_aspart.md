# Insulin Aspart: From Diabetes Mellitus to Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

## One-Sentence Summary

Insulin aspart is a rapid-acting insulin analogue, originally used to treat diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus**,
with **31 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction. Note that this is essentially a confirmation of an established use rather than a true repurposing case.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available in the compiled data. Based on known information, insulin aspart is a rapid-acting human insulin analogue that activates the insulin receptor to lower blood glucose. Its efficacy in diabetes mellitus is well established.

Type 1 diabetes is characterised by absolute insulin deficiency, so insulin replacement is a direct, mechanistically sound treatment. Because the original indication (diabetes mellitus) already overlaps with type 1 diabetes, this prediction confirms an established use rather than revealing a new therapeutic direction.

The other nine predictions in the ranking (for example autoimmune oophoritis, opsismodysplasia, stiff person syndrome and lipodystrophies) have no clinical trials and little or no mechanistic support. Some, such as drug-induced localised lipodystrophy, describe known adverse effects of injected insulin, not treatment targets. Two (permanent neonatal diabetes and pancreatic agenesis) involve insulin deficiency and are mechanistically plausible, but evidence is limited to indirect literature.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information. No warnings, contraindications or drug interaction records were found in the compiled data. Hypoglycaemia monitoring is the key guardrail for any insulin therapy, and repeated injection at the same site is a recognised cause of localised lipodystrophy.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

Multiple completed Phase 3 trials and systematic reviews support insulin aspart in type 1 diabetes, and the mechanism is direct insulin replacement.
