# Aripiprazole: From Bipolar Disorder and Schizophrenia to Major Affective Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Aripiprazole is an atypical antipsychotic originally used for bipolar disorder (including manic episodes), schizophrenia and autism-related symptoms.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **major affective disorder** (major depressive disorder), with **35 clinical trials** and **20 publications** compiled for this direction, including several completed Phase 3 placebo-controlled trials.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacological knowledge, aripiprazole is a partial agonist at dopamine D2/D3 and serotonin 5-HT1A receptors, and an antagonist at 5-HT2A receptors. This profile plausibly augments serotonergic antidepressants by modulating dopaminergic and serotonergic tone in mood circuits.

Bipolar disorder and mania, the drug's original indications, sit in the same affective disorder spectrum as major depression, which supports biological plausibility. The literature also notes that several atypical antipsychotics, aripiprazole among them, are used as adjuncts to antidepressants in major depressive disorder, mainly in patients who respond inadequately to antidepressants alone.

The evidence supports adjunctive use (added to an antidepressant) in patients with an inadequate response. It does not support monotherapy.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related conditions (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: Conditions flagged include neutropenia, agranulocytosis, leukopenia, neuroleptic malignant syndrome, epilepsy, diabetes mellitus, hyperglycemia, diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperlipidemia, obesity, orthostatic hypotension, myocardial infarction, coronary artery disease, heart conduction disease, sudden cardiac arrest and cerebrovascular disorder.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found. Listed examples include a **major** interaction with bupropion. **Moderate** interactions include antidiabetic agents (metformin, pioglitazone, acarbose, alogliptin, canagliflozin, albiglutide), cimetidine, famotidine, clarithromycin, cisapride, epinephrine, aprepitant, and anticholinergics (atropine, hyoscyamine). Bupropion matters here because it is a common comparator and augmentation partner in depression trials.
- **Impulse-control adverse events**: Case reports link aripiprazole to impulsive-compulsive behaviours, including trichotillomania.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications, as label text was not available for this report.

## Other Predicted Indications (Brief Note)

- **Remaining predictions** (for example gaze palsy with scoliosis, hydranencephaly, retinal dystrophy): model prediction only, with no plausible mechanism or evidence. These are likely knowledge-graph artifacts and should be held.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed, large Phase 3 placebo-controlled trials and recent network meta-analyses support adjunctive aripiprazole in major depressive disorder, which gives an L1 evidence level. However, all New Zealand licences are cancelled, and label-based safety information and approved indication text are missing, so this cannot proceed without further checks.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Medsafe package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Confirmation of the current regulatory status in New Zealand, including whether a new licence or other supply route exists
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A safety monitoring plan covering metabolic parameters, neutropenia, impulse-control behaviours and interactions (notably bupropion)
- Restriction of use to adjunctive therapy in patients with inadequate antidepressant response, consistent with the trial evidence
