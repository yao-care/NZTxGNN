## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, prednisolone is a glucocorticoid, its efficacy in autoimmune and inflammatory disease has been established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to alopecia areata.

Alopecia areata is a T-cell-mediated autoimmune attack on hair follicles. Prednisolone's glucocorticoid immunosuppression (reduced pro-inflammatory cytokines and cytotoxic T-cell activity) matches this pathology, and the disease falls within the drug's autoimmune indication area. A small study also reported that oral pulse steroids lowered serum and tissue TNF-α in alopecia areata, which suggests a plausible mechanism.

There are limits. Relapse after withdrawal is common, and long-term systemic steroid toxicity restricts use, particularly in children. JAK inhibitors are now preferred for severe disease.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease associations from the knowledge graph (not label text)**: The graph links prednisolone with contraindication to hypertension, epilepsy, hyperglycaemia, congestive heart failure, herpes simplex virus keratitis, measles, inherited porphyria, certain urea cycle disorders (such as citrullinemia and hyperargininemia), and pituitary hormone deficiency conditions. Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available for this report; please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
A placebo-controlled RCT, a Cochrane network meta-analysis and systematic reviews support oral pulse prednisolone in alopecia areata, but there are no registered trials. Relapse after withdrawal and steroid toxicity limit its use, and JAK inhibitors are preferred for severe disease. Neither New Zealand licence is currently valid.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Medsafe package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A pulse dosing regimen and a monitoring plan covering growth, bone density, infection and glucose, particularly in children
- A decision on how it positions against JAK inhibitors
- Confirmation of a currently available product, since both licences are cancelled
