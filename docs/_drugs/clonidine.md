# Clonidine: From Hypertension to Tourette Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Clonidine is a central alpha-2 adrenergic agonist, originally used for hypertension, open-angle glaucoma and attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Tourette syndrome**, the best-supported of its top-ranked candidates,
with **2 clinical trials** and **19 publications** currently supporting this direction, including guideline-level and randomized-trial evidence.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, clonidine is an alpha-2 adrenergic agonist. It reduces noradrenergic output from the central nervous system, which lowers sympathetic tone and blood pressure.

Tourette syndrome and ADHD frequently occur together, and clonidine is already used for ADHD. Noradrenergic hyperarousal is thought to worsen tic expression, impulsivity and aggression, so dampening it is a plausible way to reduce tics and comorbid ADHD symptoms. Clonidine is recognized as a first-line pharmacological option for tics, particularly when ADHD is present, in European guidelines and in network meta-analyses.

Other candidates are far weaker. Mania, migraine and obesity have limited or indirect evidence. Faciodigitogenital syndrome, chondromyxoid fibroma, hypervitaminosis and 16p11.2 microdeletion syndrome have no supporting trials or literature, and no mechanistic link has been identified.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph (not label text):** The knowledge graph links clonidine to several conditions that may warrant caution. These include hypotensive disorder, myocardial infarction and myocardial ischemia, heart conduction disease, sudden cardiac arrest, type 1 and type 2 diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, liver failure, pancreatitis, anuria, respiratory failure, systemic lupus erythematosus, gout and several mineral or metabolic disorders. These need verification against the product label.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified, all of the moderate level among the 20 listed. They include corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, triamcinolone), morphine, bupropion, dronabinol, insulin products, sulfonylureas (glimepiride, glipizide, glyburide, chlorpropamide, acetohexamide) and SGLT2 inhibitors (canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin).
- **Practical guardrails:** Monitor blood pressure and heart rate and watch for sedation. Avoid abrupt discontinuation because of the risk of rebound hypertension. Reviews also note possible metabolic effects such as weight gain and hyperglycemia.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Tourette syndrome has guideline endorsement, a network meta-analysis and a 2024 RCT of the clonidine patch supporting clonidine use, which is consistent with its established role in ADHD. The two registered trials are small Phase 4 studies, and all New Zealand authorizations are cancelled, so any use would need to be a new application.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Confirmation of the regulatory pathway, since there is currently no valid New Zealand authorization
- A review of the pooled evidence, including the 2024 patch RCT, to confirm efficacy and tolerability in the target population
- A blood pressure, heart rate and sedation monitoring plan, with guidance on gradual discontinuation

Other candidates (mania, migraine, obesity) should stay as research questions. The remaining predictions (faciodigitogenital syndrome, chondromyxoid fibroma, hypervitaminosis, 16p11.2 microdeletion syndrome, trichotillomania, specific developmental disorder) should be held, because there is little or no supporting evidence.
