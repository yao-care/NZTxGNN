## One-Sentence Summary

Tenofovir disoproxil is a nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitor originally used to treat chronic hepatitis B virus infection, HIV infectious disease and AIDS.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, tenofovir disoproxil is a prodrug of tenofovir, a nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitor. Its efficacy in HIV and hepatitis B has been established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to feline immunodeficiency virus (FIV).

FIV is a lentivirus that causes progressive immune dysfunction in cats, similar to HIV in humans. Because FIV also relies on a reverse transcriptase, the mechanistic plausibility is reasonable. The one related study (2023) tested a combination antiretroviral regimen (dolutegravir, tenofovir and emtricitabine) in FIV-infected cats. It is a combination study, so it cannot isolate the contribution of tenofovir.

An important caveat is that this is a veterinary condition. It is not a new human indication, so its value for human drug repurposing is limited. The same prediction pattern also appears for simian immunodeficiency virus infection. The macaque studies show that tenofovir disoproxil, alone or with emtricitabine, gives partial to full protection as prophylaxis, but they mainly support the existing HIV indication rather than a new one.

## Safety Considerations

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report, and no drug interaction records were found. Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

The knowledge graph does list the following conditions as contraindication-related for this drug. They are not label text and should be verified against the official product information:

- **Others:** epilepsy, anxiety and neurotic disorders, dysthymic disorder, delusional disorder, paranoid schizophrenia, gallbladder disease, hypertriglyceridemia, obesity

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The predicted indication is a veterinary condition supported by only one indirect, preclinical combination-therapy study, with no clinical trials. The TxGNN score is high, but it is not matched by independent evidence of a new human use. The lower-ranked predictions (a rare neurodevelopmental disorder and an obsolete familial combined hyperlipidaemia term) have no literature and no plausible mechanism.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Medsafe package insert warnings and contraindications (currently a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A decision on whether a veterinary indication falls within the scope of this repurposing programme
- If pursued, FIV studies that test tenofovir alone, rather than within a combination regimen
- Clarification of the regulatory pathway, given that both New Zealand licences are cancelled
