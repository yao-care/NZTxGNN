## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Cinchocaine is known as an amide-type local anaesthetic that blocks voltage-gated sodium channels. In theory, a topical or lozenge-type anaesthetic effect could dampen airway sensory nerve activity and the cough reflex.

However, no clinical or published evidence supports this for bronchitis. The link rests only on the TxGNN prediction score, and the local products are rectal ointments and suppositories rather than airway formulations. The prediction should be treated as a hypothesis, not as a finding.
