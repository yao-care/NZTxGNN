## Polymyxin B: Bronchitis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Polymyxin B is an antibiotic active against Gram-negative bacteria. In New Zealand it was licensed only in neomycin-containing eye ointment and eye drops (Maxitrol), and both licenses are now cancelled.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **bronchitis** with a very high score, but **no clinical trials** and only **12 publications** (mostly small, old, preclinical or safety-focused) are available.
The evidence is weak, and some of it points to a safety concern with inhaled use.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Polymyxin B is a cationic lipopeptide antibiotic. It is known to disrupt the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria. This provides a plausible link to bacterial tracheobronchitis or lower respiratory infections caused by multidrug-resistant Gram-negative organisms such as *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and *Acinetobacter baumannii*.

Its known use is topical and ophthalmic, so the connection to bronchitis is indirect. It rests on the antibacterial mechanism being applicable to airway infection, and on published reports of systemic or inhaled polymyxin B in respiratory infections. Several publications suggest caution. Inhaled polymyxin B can cause bronchoconstriction and has been used as a non-specific bronchial provocation agent in asthma and chronic bronchitis. Eosinophil-related findings come from animal models, where polymyxin B is used to induce eosinophilic bronchitis, so they do not support a therapeutic effect.

The high TxGNN score is therefore not backed by clinical trial data.

## Safety Considerations

- **Respiratory risk with inhalation**: Several publications describe bronchoconstriction after inhaled polymyxin B, and the drug has been used as a bronchial provocation agent in asthma and chronic bronchitis.
- **Hypersensitivity**: A case report describes anaphylaxis to polymyxin B-trimethoprim eye drops.
- **Drug interactions**: 73 interactions were identified. Those classified as **Major** include:
  - Botulinum toxin type A
  - Amikacin
  - Cisatracurium
  - Cidofovir
  - Deferasirox
  - Iothalamic acid
  - Diatrizoate
  - Intravenous human immunoglobulin G
  - Human botulinum neurotoxin A/B immune globulin
  - Human cytomegalovirus immune globulin
  - Live cholera vaccine (*Vibrio cholerae* CVD 103-HgR strain)

  Moderate interactions include the estrogens (ethinylestradiol, estradiol, mestranol), mesalazine, balsalazide, ibandronate, capreomycin, picosulfuric acid and clofarabine.

Please refer to the package insert for label warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a high model score, without any registered clinical trial. The literature is small, old, and largely preclinical or safety-focused, and it raises a bronchoconstriction concern with inhaled use.


**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (not currently available)
- Controlled clinical data on inhaled or systemic polymyxin B for bronchitis, including a bronchoconstriction safety assessment
- Verification of whether a suitable product and route could be marketed in New Zealand, given that both licenses are cancelled
- For conjunctivitis, confirmation of whether this is already a label use and a review of the contribution of polymyxin B alone
