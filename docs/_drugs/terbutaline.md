## Terbutaline: From Asthma to Obstructive Lung Disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Terbutaline is a short-acting bronchodilator originally used for asthma, bronchitis and pulmonary emphysema.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **obstructive lung disease**, with **14 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction. Most of the trials use terbutaline as a comparator or reliever, so this is better read as confirmation of an existing use than as true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on established pharmacology, terbutaline is a selective beta2-adrenergic agonist. It relaxes bronchial smooth muscle and produces bronchodilation.

Obstructive lung disease covers asthma, chronic bronchitis, emphysema and COPD. Terbutaline's original indications (asthma, bronchitis, pulmonary emphysema) already fall within this group. The prediction therefore largely confirms an existing use. Reviews and trials in the literature also describe terbutaline use in COPD.

The market status is "Cancelled only", so product availability must be verified before any further development.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications / cautions (not label text)**: hypertension, epilepsy, hyperglycaemia, diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, potassium deficiency, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease, myocardial ischaemia, coronary artery disease, respiratory failure, pulmonary oedema, hyperthyroidism (Graves disease, thyrotoxicosis) and placental abruption.
- **Drug Interactions**: 407 interactions were identified. Examples:
  - Moderate: epinephrine, antidiabetic agents (acarbose, alogliptin, pioglitazone, canagliflozin, chlorpropamide, albiglutide), clarithromycin, famotidine, loperamide, isometheptene, and laxatives or bowel preparations (bisacodyl, picosulfuric acid, polyethylene glycol).
  - Minor: corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, budesonide, beclomethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone).
- A beta-blocker interaction study in COPD patients with cardiac disease is also in the literature (PMID 6107217).

Please refer to the package insert for label warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several large completed Phase 3 trials in asthma include terbutaline as an active or reliever arm, and COPD studies support its bronchodilator effect. However, the evidence is mostly indirect, the use overlaps the existing indications, and the only New Zealand authorization is cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmation of current product availability and regulatory status in New Zealand
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (not yet retrieved)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A safety monitoring plan for patients with cardiovascular disease, diabetes, hyperthyroidism or hypokalaemia risk
