# Pegaspargase: From Acute Lymphoblastic Leukaemia to Precursor Lymphoblastic Lymphoma/Leukaemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Pegaspargase is a PEGylated form of L-asparaginase, originally used to treat acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (ALL).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **precursor lymphoblastic lymphoma/leukaemia**, with **33 clinical trials** and **19 publications** currently linked to this prediction. However, this is essentially a confirmation of its existing indication rather than a true new use, because the predicted disease is closely related to the original one.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known information, pegaspargase is a PEGylated L-asparaginase that depletes circulating asparagine. Lymphoblasts have low asparagine synthetase expression and depend on asparagine from the blood, so depleting it causes selective leukaemic cell death.

The predicted indication is the same disease family as the original one (precursor lymphoblastic leukaemia and lymphoma are the same biological entity at different sites of presentation). The mechanism is therefore the same as in the established ALL use. This is not a true repurposing case; it supports the original indication. Pegaspargase is a standard part of multi-agent backbones in large Phase 3 trials, including COG AALL1231, AALL0434 and ALLR3.

Other predictions in the data are much weaker. Lymphoid neoplasm has indirect, mainly ALL-based support. Hodgkin lymphoma, blast-phase CML, CLL/SLL, follicular lymphoma and cblE deficiency have little or no supporting evidence (see the note at the end).

## Safety Considerations

- **Reported toxicities (from the literature):** hepatotoxicity, pancreatitis, thrombosis, hypertriglyceridaemia, hyperglycaemia and hypersensitivity reactions. Risks appear higher in adults, particularly those aged 55 or older, and in obese patients.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were recorded, all of those listed being Moderate severity. Examples include corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, budesonide), glucose-lowering drugs (metformin, acarbose, alogliptin, albiglutide, dulaglutide, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, saxagliptin, chlorpropamide), acetylsalicylic acid, tetracyclines (doxycycline, tetracycline), clarithromycin, bupropion and chenodeoxycholic acid.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The predicted indication is effectively the drug's original use, and it is backed by several large completed Phase 3 trials and RCTs in ALL where pegaspargase is part of the regimen. The main limits are that its individual contribution cannot be separated from the combinations, and that the New Zealand licence is cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (currently unavailable, so formal safety screening cannot be completed)
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of product availability in New Zealand, since only a cancelled licence exists
- A toxicity monitoring plan (liver, pancreas, lipids, glucose, coagulation, hypersensitivity), with extra caution in patients aged 55 or older

**Note on other predictions:** Lymphoid neoplasm (L1 for the ALL component only; non-randomised data for other subtypes) could also proceed with guardrails, limited to ALL. Hodgkin lymphoma and blast-phase CML have only indirect evidence (L4) and should be held. CLL/SLL (including its subtypes), follicular lymphoma and methylcobalamin deficiency cblE have no trials or literature (L5) and no plausible asparagine-dependency rationale, so they should be held.
