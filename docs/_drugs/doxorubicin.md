# Doxorubicin: From Lymphoma and Leukaemia to Ewing Sarcoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Doxorubicin is an anthracycline chemotherapy drug, originally used for lymphomas (Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin) and acute lymphoblastic leukaemia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Ewing sarcoma**,
with **29 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction, including several large Phase 3 randomised trials in which doxorubicin is part of the standard regimen.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Doxorubicin is a topoisomerase II inhibitor and DNA intercalator. It acts on rapidly dividing tumour cells, and its pharmacological target in the data compiled for this report is DNA topoisomerase II alpha (TOP2A). Detailed mechanism of action data from the drug database is not available, so this description is based on the mechanistic rationale compiled for the report.

Doxorubicin is already used in lymphoma and leukaemia regimens, and it is a backbone of the VDC/IE regimen for Ewing sarcoma (vincristine, doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide, alternating with ifosfamide and etoposide). Ewing sarcoma is a fast-growing cancer of bone and soft tissue, mainly in children, adolescents and young adults. Cytotoxic drugs that damage DNA in dividing cells are therefore mechanistically suitable.

Note that for Ewing sarcoma, doxorubicin is already part of standard multi-agent therapy. The prediction is thus supported more by established clinical practice than by a new, doxorubicin-specific signal. Most trials test the whole regimen rather than doxorubicin alone.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related conditions (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: liver failure, liver disease, hyperbilirubinaemia, heart disease, cardiomyopathy, congestive heart failure, heart conduction disease, sudden cardiac arrest, neutropenia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, anaemia, agranulocytosis, and infectious or fungal disease. These come from a knowledge graph and should be confirmed against the official package insert.
- **Drug interactions**: no drug-drug interactions were identified. The only interaction record is pharmacological, listing DNA topoisomerase II alpha (TOP2A) as the molecular target.

For label-based warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert, which could not be obtained for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 3 randomised trials and several RCT publications place doxorubicin within standard multi-agent therapy for Ewing sarcoma (evidence level L1). However, the evidence supports the combination regimen rather than doxorubicin alone, and no doxorubicin product is currently licensed in New Zealand.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Medsafe package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking data gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from the drug database
- Confirmation of access to a licensed or unapproved-medicine supply route in New Zealand, since all licences are cancelled
- A cardiac monitoring plan covering cumulative anthracycline dose and cardioprotection (such as dexrazoxane)
- Clarification of doxorubicin's own contribution within the VDC/IE regimen
