## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Ciprofloxacin is a fluoroquinolone antibiotic, and such drugs generally act by inhibiting bacterial DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV.

Two routes could link it to diffuse scleroderma:

1. **Treating small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO).** SIBO is common in systemic sclerosis and causes diarrhoea, weight loss and malabsorption. A 1995 study (PMID 7728404) examined SIBO detection and treatment outcome in 24 patients with systemic sclerosis, of whom six had the diffuse form. This route relieves symptoms only and does not change the underlying disease.
2. **A proposed antifibrotic effect in skin.** A 2010 report (PMID 20507401) described a controlled, double-blind randomised trial of oral ciprofloxacin in scleroderma skin. Only a truncated abstract is available, so the results cannot be confirmed here, and this mechanism remains hypothetical.

The very high TxGNN score is not backed by confirmed controlled clinical data in the material reviewed.
