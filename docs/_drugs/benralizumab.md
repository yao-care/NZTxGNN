# Benralizumab: From Eosinophilic Pneumonia to Thrombocytopenia Due to Immune Destruction

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on pharmacology information, benralizumab is an antibody that binds the IL-5 receptor alpha subunit (IL5RA). This depletes eosinophils and basophils through antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity. It is approved in the US for severe eosinophilic asthma and, since September 2024, for eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis.

**Thrombocytopenia due to immune destruction (rank 1):** The TxGNN score is very high, but no link between eosinophil depletion and immune thrombocytopenia is established in the available data. Any rationale, such as modulating Th2-skewed immune dysregulation, would be speculative. This is a purely computational prediction.

**Dermatitis (rank 2):** Eosinophils and basophils contribute to Th2-driven inflammation in atopic dermatitis and other eosinophil-associated skin disease, which gives a plausible mechanistic link. However, the largest trial (HILLIER) was terminated, and a published report describes a lack of effect on signs and symptoms of moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis. This weakens the case considerably.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked prediction, thrombocytopenia due to immune destruction, rests on a model score alone (L5), with no trials, no literature and no established mechanistic link. The best-supported alternative, dermatitis, has three Phase 2 trials (L2). However, the largest was terminated and its published results show a lack of effect. In addition, the only New Zealand licence is cancelled, so there is no current local market access.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (from Medsafe), which are required before any safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data (from DrugBank)
- The reason for termination of NCT04605094, and a review of the HILLIER trial results
- Any mechanistic or clinical evidence linking eosinophil or basophil depletion to immune thrombocytopenia, before further investment in that prediction
- The regulatory and supply pathway in New Zealand, given the cancelled licence
