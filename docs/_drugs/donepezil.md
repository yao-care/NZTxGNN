## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, donepezil is a selective acetylcholinesterase inhibitor (the pharmacology data show it acts on the ACHE target and is about 188-fold more selective for acetylcholinesterase than butyrylcholinesterase). By blocking the breakdown of acetylcholine, it raises central cholinergic tone. Its efficacy in Alzheimer disease is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to movement disorders.

**Top-ranked prediction (psychogenic movement disorders):** No trials or literature were found. A cholinergic link to functional movement disorders is speculative and not supported by the data, so this prediction rests on the model score alone.

**Chronic tic disorder (best-supported candidate):** Striatal cholinergic dysfunction has been proposed in tics and ADHD. Mouse studies (PMIDs 14643839, 16045972) suggest donepezil modulates serotonergic and cholinergic circuits in a head-twitch model used to mimic tics. Small clinical reports and an 18-week open-label study in children and adolescents with tics and ADHD point the same way. The evidence is limited and mostly uncontrolled.

**Other predictions in the list:**
- *Extrapyramidal and movement disease* (L3) and *lingual-facial-buccal dyskinesia* (L4): cholinergic augmentation may correct the striatal dopamine-acetylcholine imbalance, and one report describes benefit in tardive movement disorders. However, a 2025 systematic review and several case reports and pharmacovigilance studies describe donepezil-associated movement disorders (such as Pisa syndrome and jaw tremor), so the direction of effect is uncertain.
- *Primary orthostatic tremor*, *benign shuddering attacks*, *tremor-nystagmus-duodenal ulcer syndrome* and *benign paroxysmal tonic upgaze of childhood with ataxia*: prediction only, with no direct supporting evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications and cautions flagged in the knowledge graph** (not taken from package insert text): epilepsy, asthma (including intrinsic and allergic asthma), bladder neck obstruction, active peptic ulcer disease, sick sinus syndrome (including the familial form), and atrioventricular block.
- **Pharmacological target:** donepezil acts on acetylcholinesterase (ACHE), and this is the only interaction entry recorded.
- **Predicted-indication concerns:** increased cholinergic activity may aggravate duodenal ulcer, and donepezil has been associated with drug-induced movement disorders (Pisa syndrome, jaw tremor). Paediatric safety would need evaluation for chronic tic disorder.

Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (top-ranked prediction, psychogenic movement disorders); Proceed with Guardrails as a research question for chronic tic disorder**

**Rationale:**
- The top-ranked prediction has a high model score but no trials or literature, so it cannot be advanced. Chronic tic disorder has the most consistent supporting signal (an open-label paediatric study, case reports, and mouse models), but it is small, uncontrolled and has no registered trials. Movement-disorder predictions also carry a conflicting safety signal.
- Both New Zealand licences are cancelled, so there is currently no marketed product in the country.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Medsafe (currently missing and blocking safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of the design and outcomes of the tic and ADHD studies (PMIDs 18343255, 10440471, 16986157)
- A controlled trial design for chronic tic disorder, with a paediatric safety plan covering gastrointestinal, cardiac (bradycardia, conduction block) and extrapyramidal effects
- Clarification of the regulatory pathway, since the existing licences are cancelled
