# Papaverine: From Peripheral Vascular Disease to Ischemic Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Papaverine is a smooth muscle relaxant and vasodilator, originally used for peripheral vascular disease, intermittent claudication, peripheral arterial disease, brain ischemia and transient ischemic attack.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **ischemic disease**,
but the supporting evidence is thin: **1 registered clinical trial** (an observational registry that does not test papaverine as a treatment) and **20 publications**, mostly reviews, procedural reports and case studies.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank is not available for this report. Pharmacology data compiled for this report show that papaverine inhibits phosphodiesterases (PDE3A, PDE3B and PDE10A), which raises cAMP/cGMP in vascular smooth muscle and causes vasodilation. Clinically, it has been used as an antispasmodic in the gastrointestinal tract and as a vasodilator of cerebral, coronary and penile blood vessels.

The predicted indication is consistent with the original ones. Peripheral arterial disease, brain ischemia and transient ischemic attack are all conditions of insufficient blood supply, and a drug that relaxes vascular smooth muscle could plausibly improve perfusion. The literature shows supportive uses such as intra-arterial infusion in non-occlusive mesenteric ischemia, spinal cord protection during aortic surgery, and intracoronary use to measure coronary flow reserve. An animal study also reports a reduced infarct volume after cerebral ischemia.

However, these are mostly procedural or diagnostic uses, not randomized trial evidence that papaverine treats ischemic disease. "Ischemic disease" is also a very broad category, so the prediction is best treated as a research question rather than a confirmed new use.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related conditions (from the drug-disease knowledge graph, not label text)**: liver disease, gallbladder disease, hypotensive disorder, coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, coronary thrombosis, hemoglobinopathy, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease, glaucoma, atrioventricular block. Several of these (coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, heart conduction disease) overlap with the cardiovascular population most relevant to ischemic disease, so they need careful review.
- **Pharmacological targets**: papaverine inhibits PDE3A, PDE3B and PDE10A. No drug-drug interaction severity levels are available.
- **Literature signals**: a 1975 report describes antagonism of levodopa by papaverine, and papaverine injection into the penis is associated with ischemic priapism.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high (99.91%), and the vasodilator mechanism is plausible. However, there is no randomized evidence for treating ischemic disease, the only registered trial is an observational diagnostic registry, and the product's New Zealand licence has been cancelled. The knowledge graph also lists coronary artery disease and myocardial infarction as contraindication-related conditions. The idea is a research question, not a ready-to-use new indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The Medsafe package insert, to confirm warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- A narrower, clinically defined target (for example, a specific vasospasm or perfusion setting) in place of the broad "ischemic disease" category
- Controlled clinical evidence of efficacy in that target condition
- A route and formulation plan, since the only New Zealand licence was for an injection and has been cancelled
