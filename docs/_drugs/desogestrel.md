## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, desogestrel is a progestin that suppresses gonadotropin secretion. Combined with ethinylestradiol, it provides estrogen/progestin replacement.

In hypothalamic oligo-amenorrhea, for example in athletes, combined oral contraceptives can restore regular withdrawal bleeding and help preserve bone density. This is symptomatic hormonal management rather than correction of the underlying cause, and it would not restore spontaneous ovulation. The relationship to the original contraceptive use is therefore one of shared hormonal action, not a new mechanism.

Other predictions from the model (acne, breast adenosis, primary ovarian failure and others) have lower or no supporting evidence. Acne is the only other candidate with some comparative clinical literature, largely for combined pills rather than desogestrel alone.

## Safety Considerations

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

- **Disease associations from the knowledge graph (not label text)**: The model's knowledge graph links desogestrel with contraindication-type relationships to several conditions. These include hypertension, epilepsy, liver and gallbladder disease, porphyria, diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperglycaemia, hypertriglyceridaemia, hypothyroidism, systemic lupus erythematosus, asthma, hypercalcaemia, thrombophlebitis, uterine leiomyoma, female breast carcinoma, and anxiety or dysthymic disorders. These should be confirmed against the official label.
- **Drug Interactions**: 43 interactions were recorded, and the 20 shown in the data are all graded Moderate. They are mainly with glucose-lowering agents, which may change glycaemic control. Examples are metformin, several insulins (aspart, degludec, detemir, glulisine, lispro, human), GLP-1 agonists (liraglutide, lixisenatide, albiglutide), DPP-4 inhibitors (alogliptin, linagliptin), SGLT2 inhibitors (dapagliflozin, ertugliflozin), nateglinide, rosiglitazone, and metreleptin. Aprepitant and glycerol phenylbutyrate are also listed.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only related trial is indirect, and the literature consists mostly of older reviews and combined-pill studies rather than desogestrel-specific data for amenorrhea. The mechanism is plausible as symptomatic hormonal management. The sole New Zealand authorization is cancelled, and safety label data is missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Obtain the Medsafe package insert to complete warnings and contraindications review
- Obtain detailed mechanism of action data (e.g., from DrugBank)
- Run a targeted search for trials or studies directly testing desogestrel (alone or combined) in amenorrhea, especially hypothalamic amenorrhea
- Clarify the original approved indication and current regulatory pathway, since the existing authorization is cancelled
- Assess the knowledge-graph contraindication signals (cardiovascular, hepatic, metabolic) against the label before any further evaluation
