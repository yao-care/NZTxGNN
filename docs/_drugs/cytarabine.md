## Cytarabine: Small cell lung carcinoma
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Cytarabine is a pyrimidine nucleoside analog antimetabolite that interferes with DNA synthesis in rapidly dividing cells.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **small cell lung carcinoma**.
Evidence is thin: **0 registered clinical trials** and **1 small clinical study** of high-dose cytarabine in this disease (PMID 3030547), with the remaining publications concerning other diseases (mainly non-small cell lung cancer). The evidence level is **L3**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available in the data compiled for this report. Cytarabine belongs to the pyrimidine nucleoside analog class. After intracellular activation, drugs of this class interfere with DNA synthesis and are most active in cells that divide quickly.

Small cell lung carcinoma is a highly proliferative tumour, so a DNA-synthesis-directed antimetabolite is mechanistically plausible. One older study (1987) tested high-dose cytarabine in small cell lung cancer, but no abstract is available, so its results cannot be summarised.

There are also reasons for caution. Reviews note that many solid tumours are relatively insensitive to cytarabine, possibly because of high levels of the enzyme that breaks it down (PMIDs 1332622 and 12049569). Most of the lung cancer studies retrieved concern non-small cell lung cancer, a different disease. In those studies, cytarabine with cisplatin gave modest response rates and notable blood toxicity. The very high TxGNN score is therefore not matched by direct clinical evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible for a highly proliferative tumour. However, direct evidence for small cell lung carcinoma is limited to one old, small study with no available abstract. The other lung cancer studies concern a different disease, and the available data show significant blood toxicity. The high TxGNN score alone does not justify moving forward.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A full review of the 1987 small cell lung cancer study (PMID 3030547), including response rates and toxicity
- Mechanism of action data, and any preclinical evidence in small cell lung cancer models
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Medsafe
- Comparison against current standard small cell lung cancer treatments, and a safety monitoring plan for blood toxicity
