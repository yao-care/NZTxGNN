# Trimethoprim: From Urinary Tract Infection to Punctate Epithelial Keratoconjunctivitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Trimethoprim is an antibacterial originally used for urinary tract infections and several other infections, including infectious otitis media, pneumocystosis, shigellosis and conjunctivitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this specific prediction.
The second-ranked prediction, conjunctivitis, is already an original indication and is therefore not a true repurposing candidate.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, trimethoprim is a bacterial dihydrofolate reductase inhibitor. Its efficacy in bacterial infections, including ocular surface infections, is established, and it is used topically, usually combined with polymyxin B, for bacterial conjunctivitis and blepharoconjunctivitis.

The plausible link is the overlap between these ocular surface infections and punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis, which can be infectious or inflammatory. However, it is frequently viral (for example adenoviral), and an antibacterial would not be expected to address a viral cause. The mechanistic rationale is therefore unsupported at this stage.

The model score is very high, but a high score alone is not evidence of clinical benefit.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis has a high model score but no trials or literature (L5). The condition is often viral, which an antibacterial would not treat. The drug has no currently valid New Zealand licence. The conjunctivitis literature supports existing use, not this new indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Evidence on the causes of punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis and whether a bacterial subset would respond to trimethoprim
- Mechanism of action data and a clear mechanistic argument
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Medsafe
- Confirmation of whether an ophthalmic trimethoprim product (for example with polymyxin B) is marketed in New Zealand under other records, and which routes are required
- Any clinical or observational data in this specific condition
