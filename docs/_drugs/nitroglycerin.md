## Nitroglycerin: From Angina and Vasodilator Therapy to Pulmonary Hypertension
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Nitroglycerin is a nitric oxide (NO) donor and vasodilator. The New Zealand licence records compiled for this report do not state an approved indication, but the product forms (ointment, patches, sublingual spray) are consistent with cardiovascular and anal-fissure use.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **pulmonary hypertension**, with **3 clinical trials** (1 completed, small, non-phased) and **20 publications** (mostly reviews and small haemodynamic studies, plus one randomised trial in newborns) supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known pharmacology, nitroglycerin is an NO donor: NO activates soluble guanylate cyclase, raises cGMP and relaxes vascular smooth muscle. This pathway is the same one targeted by established pulmonary hypertension therapies.

Its established use as a vasodilator is mechanistically close to the new indication, since lowering pulmonary vascular resistance is the therapeutic goal. Given by nebulisation or inhalation, it may produce relatively selective pulmonary vasodilation with less systemic hypotension. Older haemodynamic studies (1983–1984) and recent paediatric and neonatal reports (2021, 2025) show acute vasodilatory effects. Durable clinical benefit has not been demonstrated.

The evidence level of L3 reflects the mix of reviews, haemodynamic studies and small trials. No completed Phase 3 RCTs exist.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (disease-level links, not label text): hypotensive disorder, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, methaemoglobinaemia, sulfhaemoglobinaemia, anaemia, macrocytic anaemia, intracerebral haemorrhage, constrictive pericarditis, restrictive cardiomyopathy and cardiac tamponade.
- **Drug Interactions**: 228 interactions were identified. Moderate-level ones include bupropion, morphine, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, dronabinol, nabilone, sapropterin and rosiglitazone. Minor-level ones include anticholinergics (atropine, hyoscyamine, scopolamine, glycopyrronium), acetylsalicylic acid and omeprazole.
- **Practical concern**: systemic vasodilation may cause hypotension. This is why inhaled delivery is being explored for pulmonary hypertension.

Please refer to the package insert for further safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible and acute haemodynamic benefit is shown in small studies, including one neonatal RCT. However, there are no completed Phase 2/3 trials, the registered trials are small or unverified, and although the drug is marketed in New Zealand (4 valid licences of 4), there is no approved route for this use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Medsafe package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Adequately powered controlled trials of nebulised or inhaled nitroglycerin with durable clinical outcomes
- A decision on formulation and route, since no inhaled product is currently licensed in New Zealand
- Clarification of the original approved indications of the cancelled licences
