# Eltrombopag: From Autoimmune Thrombocytopenia to HIV Infectious Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Eltrombopag is a thrombopoietin receptor agonist, originally used to treat autoimmune thrombocytopenia (immune thrombocytopenia).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **HIV infectious disease**, most plausibly for HIV-associated thrombocytopenia rather than as an antiviral.
Support is limited: **1 clinical trial** (terminated, and only indirectly related) and **8 publications**, mostly case reports and reviews.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, eltrombopag is a thrombopoietin receptor agonist. It stimulates megakaryocyte and progenitor cell proliferation and has proven efficacy in immune thrombocytopenia.

The link to HIV is supportive rather than antiviral. HIV infection can cause thrombocytopenia, including immune reconstitution thrombocytopenia and HIV-associated immune thrombocytopenic purpura. Case reports describe eltrombopag raising platelet counts in these settings. One report describes a trilineage response in HIV-associated aplastic anaemia, along with immunomodulatory changes (reduced Th1/Th17 cells and a higher T-regulatory cell/T-helper ratio).

The high TxGNN score most likely reflects this shared thrombocytopenia biology, not a direct effect on HIV replication. A 2020 in vitro screening study of FDA-approved drugs examined modulation of HIV-1 proviral transcription. It is preclinical and only hypothesis-generating.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-level contraindication signals (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: kidney disease, liver disease (including acute liver failure and hepatitis), gallbladder disease, anaemia, macrocytic anaemia, hyperbilirubinaemia (including hereditary forms and Gilbert syndrome), leukopenia, myelodysplastic syndrome, thrombocytosis, hepatic vein thrombosis and Budd-Chiari syndrome.
- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction query returned 100 entries; the first 20 are listed here. They are mostly Moderate-level interactions with polyvalent cation products. These include calcium salts (phosphate, acetate, carbonate, citrate, gluconate, lactate, glubionate), magnesium salts and hydroxide, aluminium hydroxide, magaldrate, kaolin, attapulgite and iron. Glyburide is also listed as Moderate. **Eluxadoline** is a **Major** interaction.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available. Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Evidence for HIV is limited to case reports, a small case series, reviews and an in vitro screening study. The single Phase 3 trial was terminated and is not HIV-specific. The plausible benefit is treating HIV-associated thrombocytopenia, not HIV itself. In New Zealand, both eltrombopag licences are cancelled, and the label safety data needed for screening are missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The Medsafe package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Clarification of the target indication (HIV-associated thrombocytopenia versus antiviral activity)
- Prospective controlled studies in HIV-associated thrombocytopenia
- Assessment of liver-related risk in HIV patients, including those with HCV/HBV co-infection, given the liver-related contraindication signals
- Review of the interaction with eluxadoline, and of cation-containing products, against antiretroviral regimens
- Confirmation of the regulatory pathway, since there is no valid New Zealand licence
