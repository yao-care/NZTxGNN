## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Lomustine is a lipophilic nitrosourea alkylating agent that crosses the blood-brain barrier. Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report, so this description rests on general pharmacological knowledge. It is cytotoxic to lymphoid malignancies and has historically been part of oral combination regimens that contain lomustine for lymphoma, including AIDS-related lymphoma and primary CNS lymphoma.

Hodgkin lymphoma is among the drug's original indications, and lymphosarcoma (non-Hodgkin lymphoma) is a closely related lymphoid cancer. This gives the prediction biological plausibility. The ability of lomustine and procarbazine to reach the central nervous system also makes these regimens relevant for CNS lymphoma.

The evidence consists mainly of multi-agent regimens, so lomustine's individual contribution cannot be isolated. Many of the publications are also veterinary studies.

Other predictions (ranks 2–10) are mostly CNS tumours, such as meningeal malignancy, spinal cord cancer, and paediatric embryonal tumours. They have weaker evidence and are noted in the conclusion.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-level cautions (knowledge graph, not label text)**: kidney disease, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, hyperbilirubinemia (including Gilbert syndrome), drug-induced liver injury, pulmonary fibrosis, and fungal or other infectious disease.
- **Drug Interactions**: the interaction screen returned 100 interacting drugs. Examples of Major interactions are **cimetidine**, **deferiprone**, **adalimumab**, and **samarium (153Sm) lexidronam**. Moderate interactions include naltrexone, mercaptopurine, zidovudine, chloramphenicol, aldesleukin, alemtuzumab, and several vaccines. Many of these are likely linked to additive myelosuppression or immunosuppression.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Lomustine has long-standing use in lymphoma regimens and a plausible mechanism, but the human trial evidence for lymphosarcoma is small (only one completed trial of meaningful size, 54 patients) and based on multi-agent combinations. The New Zealand licence is cancelled, so there is no currently marketed product, and key safety data is missing. The evidence level is L2. Among other predictions, spinal cord cancer has a Phase 3 trial in glioma, but this is by histologic extrapolation only. The rest are weaker, and several are prediction-only with no trials or literature.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data
- The package insert warnings and contraindications, and the approved indication text for the cancelled licence
- Evidence that isolates lomustine's contribution in lymphoma, as opposed to multi-drug combinations, and human rather than veterinary data
- A plan for monitoring blood counts, liver and kidney function, and lung toxicity, plus a review of the major drug interactions
