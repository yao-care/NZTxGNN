## One-Sentence Summary

Lomustine is an oral nitrosourea alkylating agent that crosses the blood-brain barrier and is used as a cytotoxic chemotherapy.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **lymphosarcoma**, with a score of 0.9990.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on what is known about the drug class, lomustine is a lipophilic nitrosourea alkylating agent. It damages the DNA of rapidly dividing cells and penetrates the central nervous system.

Lymphoid malignancies are sensitive to alkylating chemotherapy. Lomustine has historically been used in oral combination regimens for lymphoma, including AIDS-related lymphoma and CNS lymphoma. Its ability to reach the brain is relevant because some lymphomas involve the CNS. This makes the prediction biologically plausible, but the supporting studies are mostly in related lymphoma types, small, or non-human.

The one study that directly examines lymphosarcoma is a preclinical mouse study (PMID 22888657). In mice with intracranial lymphosarcoma, lomustine extended lifespan 1.6-fold, and combination with gemcitabine extended it 3.3-fold. This is encouraging but cannot be translated directly to patients.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Only a mouse study directly addresses lymphosarcoma, so the evidence level is L4. The lymphoma trials are small, mostly terminated or uninformative, and involve lomustine only within multi-drug regimens. Lomustine is marketed in New Zealand, but this prediction needs stronger direct evidence before progressing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Human studies that directly address lymphosarcoma and isolate lomustine's contribution
- Mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for a safety review
- A haematological monitoring and handling plan suitable for a cytotoxic agent
