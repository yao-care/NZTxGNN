## Lomustine: Lymphosarcoma
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Lomustine is an oral nitrosourea alkylating chemotherapy that crosses the blood-brain barrier. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **lymphosarcoma**, and the supporting evidence is limited: the only study directly on this disease is a **mouse study** (PMID 22888657), alongside related lymphoma trials and publications. The evidence level is **L4**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Lomustine is a lipophilic nitrosourea alkylating agent. It damages DNA in rapidly dividing cells, is cytotoxic to lymphoid malignancies, and reaches the central nervous system.

Historically, lomustine has been used within oral combination regimens for lymphoma, for example with procarbazine, etoposide and cyclophosphamide in AIDS-related lymphoma. These regimens exploit its oral route and its penetration into the brain. This makes activity against lymphosarcoma biologically plausible.

However, the only study of lomustine in lymphosarcoma itself is a mouse model with transplanted intracranial tumours. Gemcitabine and lomustine together extended survival in that model. The human trials and cohorts found are in related lymphomas (non-Hodgkin, CNS, AIDS-related), not in lymphosarcoma specifically, and the combination regimens make lomustine's own contribution hard to separate.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications or cautions in related records:** kidney disease, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, drug-induced liver injury, hyperbilirubinemia (including Gilbert syndrome), pulmonary fibrosis, and fungal or other infectious disease. These come from relationship records, not from label text.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were found. Major ones include cimetidine, deferiprone, adalimumab and samarium (153Sm) lexidronam. Moderate ones include naltrexone, mercaptopurine, zidovudine, chloramphenicol, aldesleukin, alemtuzumab and several vaccines. Interactions with other myelosuppressive or immunosuppressive agents deserve particular attention.

Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only study directly on lymphosarcoma is a mouse model, so the evidence level is L4. Human data come from related lymphomas, mostly small, terminated or combination-regimen studies where lomustine's own contribution is unclear. The mechanistic rationale is plausible but unproven for this disease.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Human data that directly studies lymphosarcoma with lomustine
- Mechanism of action data and the package insert warnings and contraindications
- A myelosuppression monitoring plan and review of the major drug interactions
- Clarification of how the findings in related lymphomas apply to lymphosarcoma
