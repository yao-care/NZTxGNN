# Olaparib: From Ovarian Cancer to Female Breast Carcinoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Olaparib is an oral PARP inhibitor, originally used to treat ovarian cancer (including BRCA-associated disease).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **female breast carcinoma**,
with **30 clinical trials** and **20 publications** compiled for this direction, including several randomized Phase 3 studies in BRCA-mutated breast cancer.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on published literature, olaparib inhibits PARP enzymes, which are involved in repairing DNA single-strand breaks. In tumours with homologous recombination repair deficiency, such as those with germline BRCA1/2 mutations, blocking PARP leads to accumulation of lethal DNA damage (synthetic lethality).

This is the same mechanism that underlies its activity in BRCA-associated ovarian cancer. BRCA1/2 mutations also drive a proportion of breast cancers, so the mechanism transfers directly. Benefit should be expected mainly in biomarker-selected patients (germline BRCA1/2 mutation, and possibly other homologous recombination repair gene alterations). Resistance mechanisms have also been reported, for example PCBP2-mediated stabilization of PARP1 mRNA (PMID 40773674).

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Those listed in the data include **Major** interactions with aprepitant, dexamethasone and clarithromycin. Moderate interactions include metformin, bupropion, cimetidine, miconazole, clotrimazole, ondansetron, glyburide, repaglinide, rosuvastatin and simvastatin. Prucalopride is listed as Minor. The Major interactions with aprepitant and clarithromycin are consistent with CYP3A-mediated effects, but this is an inference, not stated in the data.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report; please refer to the package insert for them.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Randomized Phase 3 trials (OlympiAD and OlympiA) support olaparib in germline BRCA-mutated breast cancer, which gives strong clinical evidence and a mechanism shared with the original ovarian indication. However, olaparib currently has no valid New Zealand authorization, benefit is limited to biomarker-selected patients, and label safety data were not available.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Medsafe, since safety screening cannot be completed without them
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A biomarker-selection plan (germline BRCA1/2 testing, and consideration of other homologous recombination repair gene alterations)
- A regulatory pathway for New Zealand, given that both licenses are cancelled
- A drug interaction management plan, especially for the Major interactions (aprepitant, dexamethasone, clarithromycin)
- Haematological monitoring plan
