## Pramipexole: Attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Pramipexole is a dopamine agonist originally used to treat **Parkinson's disease** and **restless legs syndrome**.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)**, but there are **0 registered clinical trials** and only **7 loosely related publications** for this indication, none of them an ADHD trial of pramipexole. The evidence is indirect only.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Pramipexole is a non-ergoline dopamine agonist that acts on the D2 and D3 dopamine receptors, with a preference for D3. Detailed mechanism-of-action text is not available in the data compiled for this report, but the receptor-target data confirm this D2/D3 activity.

Parkinson's disease and restless legs syndrome both involve dopaminergic signalling problems. Dopamine dysregulation is also widely implicated in ADHD. Genetic variants of the dopamine D4 receptor and the α2A adrenoceptor have been linked to ADHD, which gives the prediction a plausible mechanistic basis.

However, the link is indirect. No ADHD-specific trial of pramipexole is available. The retrieved literature is mostly off-target: restless legs syndrome reviews, single case reports, a study of a different drug (piribedil), and a receptor-biology study. The only pramipexole RCT in the set tested Tourette's syndrome, a related neurodevelopmental condition, not ADHD.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but no ADHD trial exists and the literature is indirect. The only pramipexole RCT in the set targets Tourette's syndrome. No pramipexole product is currently authorized in New Zealand. The mechanistic rationale is plausible but unproven.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (currently missing, and a blocker for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- ADHD-specific preclinical or clinical evidence for pramipexole, such as a pilot trial
- Review of whether the dopaminergic safety profile (impulse-control problems, sleepiness, orthostatic hypotension) is acceptable in an ADHD population
- Clarification of the regulatory pathway, since both New Zealand authorizations are cancelled

Among the other predicted indications for this drug, **schizophrenia** has considerably stronger evidence than ADHD: a Phase 3 add-on trial (NCT01320982, status unknown) and a published add-on RCT from 2022. It may deserve a separate evaluation.
