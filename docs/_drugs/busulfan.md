# Busulfan: From Hematologic Conditioning Use to Myelodysplastic Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Busulfan is an alkylating chemotherapy agent. The original approved indication is not recorded in the New Zealand licence data compiled for this report. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS)**, where it is used as a conditioning agent before allogeneic stem cell transplant. This direction is supported by **40 listed clinical trials** (including 2 completed Phase 3 trials in the wider evidence set) and **20 publications**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known information, busulfan is a bifunctional alkylating agent that cross-links DNA and is myeloablative. In MDS it is not a disease-modifying monotherapy. It is an established component of conditioning regimens (for example busulfan/fludarabine and busulfan/cyclophosphamide) before allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation.

In this setting busulfan helps eradicate the malignant clone and creates marrow space for donor engraftment. Allogeneic transplant is the only potentially curative therapy for eligible higher-risk MDS patients, so the prediction reflects an existing, mechanistically sensible use. It should be read as "conditioning for transplant", not as a standalone MDS drug.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (knowledge-graph associations, not label text)**: epilepsy, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, anaemia (including macrocytic anaemia and Fanconi anaemia), pulmonary fibrosis, hyperglycaemia, potassium deficiency, infectious disease (including fungal infection and dermatophytosis). These come from the TxGNN knowledge graph and need confirmation against the product label.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found in total. Of the 20 listed, the notable ones are **metronidazole (Major)**, **aprepitant (Moderate)** and **miconazole (Moderate)**; **levofloxacin** is Minor. The rest, including proton-pump inhibitors, corticosteroids, antiemetics and morphine, have no assigned severity.
- **Other literature signals**: a risk assessment of subsequent malignancy after busulfan exposure (PMID 37856098) and long-term complications in children after busulfan-based conditioning (PMID 38176654).

Package insert warnings and contraindications could not be retrieved, so please refer to the package insert for the full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several completed and ongoing Phase 2 and Phase 3 trials, plus randomised and registry studies, support busulfan-based conditioning before allogeneic transplant in MDS. The evidence supports its role as a conditioning component rather than a standalone MDS treatment, and busulfan has no valid New Zealand licence at present.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Medsafe package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of the original approved indication and a regulatory pathway, given the cancelled licence
- A safety monitoring plan for myelosuppression, hepatic toxicity and seizure risk, including seizure prophylaxis and interaction review (for example metronidazole)
- Restriction of use to transplant centres with experience in conditioning regimens
