# Ezetimibe: From Hyperlipidaemia to Hyperlipoproteinemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Ezetimibe is a cholesterol-lowering drug, originally used for hyperlipidaemia, familial hypercholesterolaemia (including the homozygous form) and sitosterolemia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **hyperlipoproteinemia**, with **41 clinical trials** and **20 publications** compiled for this prediction. However, this condition largely overlaps with the drug's existing indications, so it is not a true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, ezetimibe inhibits NPC1L1-mediated cholesterol absorption in the small intestine, which lowers LDL cholesterol and works additively with statins.

Hyperlipoproteinemia is essentially the same disease spectrum as the drug's established indications. The prediction therefore mostly confirms an existing use rather than opening a new therapeutic area. The same reasoning applies to the second-ranked prediction, familial hypercholesterolaemia: homozygous FH is already an original indication, so the possible extension is to heterozygous FH.

Two lower-ranked predictions are much weaker:
- **Hypercholesterolemia due to cholesterol 7alpha-hydroxylase deficiency** (score 99.25%): the mechanism is plausible, but the only support is one preclinical mouse study showing that NPC1L1 deficiency alters ileal FGF15 expression and bile acid pool size. There are no clinical trials. Evidence level L4, decision Hold.
- **Cholesterol-ester transfer protein deficiency** (score 99.09%): the link is weak, since NPC1L1 inhibition acts through absorption rather than CETP/HDL pathways. The literature does not address ezetimibe in this condition, and the high score appears to reflect general lipid-pathway proximity. Evidence level L5, decision Hold.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (knowledge-graph associations, not label text)**: pancreatitis, liver disease, gallbladder disease, myopathy and other muscle diseases (including skeletal muscle disease, familial periodic paralysis and rhabdomyolysis), substance abuse/dependence, nodular fasciitis.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were recorded. Those rated Moderate are chenodeoxycholic acid, eluxadoline, rosuvastatin and simvastatin. Many others are of unknown severity, including calcitriol, pantoprazole, glimepiride, mesalazine, doxycycline, clotrimazole, acarbose, morphine, metformin, omeprazole, palonosetron, rosiglitazone, lansoprazole, cimetidine, vancomycin and lactulose.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available for this report. Please refer to the package insert for authoritative safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 3 trials support ezetimibe's LDL-C-lowering effect in hyperlipidaemia and familial hypercholesterolaemia, giving an L1 evidence level. However, the predicted indication largely overlaps with existing uses, so this is confirmation rather than true repurposing. The two rare-disease predictions (CYP7A1 deficiency and CETP deficiency) should be on Hold, as they rest on preclinical or indirect evidence only.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Verify New Zealand regulatory status, since the only licence is cancelled
- Obtain the Medsafe package insert to confirm warnings, contraindications and approved indications
- Obtain detailed mechanism of action data
- Review the knowledge-graph contraindications (liver disease, myopathy, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease) against the label, and check statin co-administration risks
