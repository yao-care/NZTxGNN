## One-Sentence Summary

Acetylcysteine (N-acetylcysteine, NAC) is a thiol-based mucolytic and antioxidant agent.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **thrombotic disease**, with **7 clinical trials** and **20 publications** compiled for this direction.
Most of the evidence concerns thrombotic microangiopathies (TTP and transplant-associated TMA) and immune thrombocytopenia, rather than thrombotic disease in general.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. The following rationale comes from the trial and literature evidence.

NAC is a thiol that can reduce disulfide bonds in von Willebrand factor (VWF) multimers. This may reduce VWF–platelet binding and microthrombus formation. Preclinical mouse and baboon models of TTP support this idea (PMID 28011677). NAC also has antioxidant effects, which may lessen the prothrombotic state seen in renal insufficiency (the RENACTIF trial).

The "thrombotic disease" label is broad. The supporting evidence is concentrated in TTP and TA-TMA. Several trials are in immune thrombocytopenia (ITP), which is a platelet disorder rather than a thrombotic one, so they are only indirectly relevant.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
A completed Phase 3 trial (NCT03252925) and an active Phase 2/3 trial (NCT07279610) study NAC directly in TA-TMA. Two recent systematic reviews cover TTP, and preclinical models support the mechanism. However, outcome data are not provided, the evidence is narrow (TTP and TMA rather than thrombotic disease broadly), and only one Phase 3 trial is listed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Published outcomes of NCT03252925 and the results of NCT07279610
- A defined target population (for example TA-TMA or TTP) rather than "thrombotic disease"
- Mechanism of action data and the package insert warnings and contraindications
- A safety review of NAC given alongside anticoagulants, antiplatelet agents and plasma exchange
