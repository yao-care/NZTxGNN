## One-Sentence Summary

Polyethylene glycol (PEG) is a hydrophilic polymer originally used as an osmotic laxative for constipation and as a lubricant in eye drops for dry eye.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma**,

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, polyethylene glycol is a hydrophilic polymer used as an osmotic laxative, as an ophthalmic lubricant, and as a vehicle in topical products. Its efficacy in constipation and dry eye is established, and it may, in a limited way, be applicable to a skin condition through its moisturising properties.

A speculative rationale is that PEG has humectant and emollient-like properties, so it could in principle support skin barrier hydration in ichthyosis. This has not been validated. Congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma is a genetic keratinisation disorder (for example, variants in TGM1, ALOXE3, ALOX12B or NIPAL4) driven by defects in the skin barrier and lipid metabolism, not by dehydration alone. At most, PEG could be a symptomatic or vehicle-level contributor, not a disease-modifying agent. The model's specificity for this particular disease is not explained by this reasoning.

The oral osmotic laxative and ophthalmic lubricant uses have no evident mechanistic connection to this skin disease.
