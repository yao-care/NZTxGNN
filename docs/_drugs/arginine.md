## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known biology, L-arginine is the substrate for neuronal nitric oxide synthase (nNOS), which produces nitric oxide (NO). NO is the main inhibitory neurotransmitter driving relaxation of the gastric fundus and pyloric sphincter.

Loss of nNOS and impaired nitrergic signalling in myenteric neurons are recognised features of diabetic and other forms of gastroparesis. A mouse study reported that glucocorticoids induce gastroparesis through depletion of L-arginine, which suggests that replenishing the substrate could be a plausible approach. A rat Parkinson's disease model also shows impaired nitrergic relaxation in the pyloric sphincter, consistent with the same pathway.

However, this evidence is indirect and preclinical. None of the cited studies tests arginine in people with gastroparesis, so the high score is a computational prediction and not clinical proof.
