## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Diazepam is a benzodiazepine and a positive allosteric modulator of GABA-A receptors. By strengthening inhibitory GABA signalling, it produces sedative and hypnotic effects, which is a plausible, well-understood basis for treating insomnia.

Other benzodiazepines such as triazolam have documented hypnotic efficacy. Diazepam, however, has a long half-life and an active metabolite (nordiazepam). This raises concerns about next-day sedation, tolerance, dependence and rebound insomnia after withdrawal. The supplied literature is mostly about the drug class or tangential topics (rebound insomnia, a triazolam review, herbal medicine), so the mechanistic link is reasonable but not proven for diazepam itself.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions are recorded. Examples:
  - **Major**: morphine (additive central nervous system and respiratory depression).
  - **Moderate**: bupropion, aprepitant, omeprazole, esomeprazole, cimetidine, clarithromycin, cisapride, clotrimazole, dronabinol, metoclopramide and miconazole.
  - **Minor**: dexamethasone, betamethasone and several antacids.
- **Class-related concerns in the literature**: rebound insomnia on withdrawal, tolerance, dependence, next-day sedation, and an association between long-term benzodiazepine use and breast cancer risk.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible and one double-blind trial exists, but it is a 1981 comparison against another benzodiazepine in which diazepam performed worse. The rest of the evidence is class-level or about safety. Newer hypnotics are generally preferred over long-acting benzodiazepines because of dependence and sedation risks.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Placebo-controlled or modern comparative trials of diazepam in insomnia
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications
- A risk assessment for dependence, next-day sedation and use in older adults
