# Atorvastatin: From Hyperlipidaemia and Atherosclerotic Disease to Familial Hypercholesterolaemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Atorvastatin is a statin originally used for lipid disorders (including hypertriglyceridaemia, homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia and combined hyperlipidaemia) and for stroke and atherosclerosis prevention.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **familial hypercholesterolaemia**, with **14 clinical trials** and **19 publications** compiled for this direction.
Because familial hypercholesterolaemia is already among its listed original indications, this is effectively an on-label or near-label use rather than true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on established pharmacology, atorvastatin inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, the key enzyme in cholesterol synthesis. This upregulates hepatic LDL receptors and lowers LDL cholesterol.

The listed original indications already include homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia, so the prediction largely restates a known use. Efficacy in heterozygous disease is well established and supported by guidelines. In homozygous disease, response is limited by residual LDL receptor function, so add-on therapy (for example ezetimibe or PCSK9-directed agents) is often required. This is consistent with the trial set, where many studies test atorvastatin combined with another agent.

## Safety Considerations

- **Conditions linked to contraindication or caution (knowledge graph, not label text)**: liver failure and liver disease, kidney disease, gallbladder disease, hypothyroidism (including myxoedema and congenital hypothyroidism), myopathy and other muscle diseases, rhabdomyolysis, familial periodic paralysis, hypotensive disorder, substance abuse/dependence, and injury.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Examples:
  - **Major**: clarithromycin.
  - **Moderate**: aprepitant, omeprazole and other proton pump inhibitors (esomeprazole, lansoprazole, pantoprazole), cimetidine, clotrimazole, miconazole, dexamethasone, metronidazole, naltrexone, eliglustat, eluxadoline, pectin, rosuvastatin and simvastatin.
  - **Minor**: aluminium hydroxide, magnesium hydroxide and liraglutide.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available for this report, so please refer to the current package insert for authoritative safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several Phase 3 trials and guideline-level literature support atorvastatin in familial hypercholesterolaemia, and the condition is effectively already within its known use. However, many trials test combinations rather than atorvastatin alone, and all New Zealand licences are cancelled, so there is currently no marketed product.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Medsafe package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of which trials use atorvastatin as the studied intervention versus background therapy
- Clarification of New Zealand supply and licensing, since all licences are cancelled
- A plan for interaction management (for example CYP3A4 inhibitors such as clarithromycin) and for monitoring liver function and muscle symptoms
- Homozygous FH patients will likely need add-on therapy
