# Teriflunomide: From Multiple Sclerosis to Relapsing-Remitting Multiple Sclerosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Teriflunomide is an oral immunomodulatory drug originally used to treat multiple sclerosis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis (RRMS)**, with **13 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.
Note that RRMS is a subtype of the drug's existing indication, so this result confirms an established use rather than revealing a new one.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Teriflunomide is the active metabolite of leflunomide. It inhibits dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (DHODH), an enzyme needed for de novo pyrimidine synthesis. Activated T and B lymphocytes divide rapidly and depend on this pathway, so blocking it reduces their proliferation and dampens the inflammatory activity that drives relapses and lesion formation. Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available in the data compiled for this report, but this mechanism is well established in the published literature.

RRMS is the most common form of multiple sclerosis and is already covered by the drug's original indication. The high TxGNN score is therefore expected. It reflects an existing indication, not a true repurposing discovery.

The "Cancelled only" New Zealand market status appears to reflect a specific product record. It should be checked against current approvals before any decision is made.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Two completed Phase 3 randomized trials (a placebo-controlled study and an active-comparator study) support teriflunomide in relapsing MS. Several recent Phase 3 trials also use it as the active comparator. However, the finding confirms an existing indication, and the New Zealand licence on record is cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Verification of current New Zealand regulatory status, since the only record found is cancelled
- Package insert warnings and contraindications, which are not yet available and are needed for safety screening
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- A review of the 100 identified drug interactions, particularly the Major-level ones
