## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, flucloxacillin is a beta-lactam antibiotic of the penicillin class with activity against Gram-positive bacteria, particularly staphylococci. Its antibacterial efficacy is well established, and it is mechanistically plausible that it could act against Gram-positive pathogens that cause bacterial conjunctivitis.

However, the link is weak. The only related publication is a 2026 case report on gonococcal dacryoadenitis. That is a Gram-negative infection of the lacrimal gland, and flucloxacillin does not cover it. There is no direct evidence in conjunctivitis, and topical ocular use of flucloxacillin is not established. The high TxGNN score therefore reflects a computational association rather than clinical support.

Other top-ranked predictions have no plausible mechanism. These include rheumatoid arthritis, leprosy, Prinzmetal angina, thrombotic disorders and rare genetic syndromes. For rheumatoid arthritis, the literature concerns infections in RA patients, a methotrexate interaction study and adverse events, not treatment of RA itself.

## Safety Considerations

Literature retrieved for other predicted indications points to the following flucloxacillin-associated concerns:
- **Agranulocytosis**: a case report describes relapse after re-introduction of flucloxacillin following long-term use for staphylococcal osteomyelitis in a patient with rheumatoid arthritis.
- **Severe skin reactions**: a fatal case of Stevens-Johnson syndrome/toxic epidermal necrolysis was reported with doxycycline or flucloxacillin.
- **Drug interaction**: a small pharmacokinetic study in 10 patients found a statistically but not clinically significant decrease in methotrexate exposure with flucloxacillin.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.
