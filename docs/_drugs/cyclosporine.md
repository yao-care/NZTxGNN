# Cyclosporine: From Rheumatoid Arthritis and Dry Eye to Chronic Granulomatous Disease (Autosomal Recessive)

## One-Sentence Summary

Cyclosporine is a calcineurin-inhibiting immunosuppressant, originally used for rheumatoid arthritis, dry eye syndrome, xerophthalmia and Sjogren syndrome.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **autosomal recessive chronic granulomatous disease**, but there are **0 clinical trials** and only **1 publication** (a stem cell transplantation cohort study that does not test cyclosporine as a treatment), so the prediction is **not supported by independent clinical evidence**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Cyclosporine is known as a calcineurin inhibitor that suppresses T-cell activation, and its efficacy in immune-mediated conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis and ocular surface inflammation is established.

For chronic granulomatous disease (CGD), the link is weak. CGD is caused by a defect in phagocyte NADPH oxidase, not by overactive T-cells, so calcineurin inhibition has no direct mechanistic rationale. The only publication concerns stem cell transplantation outcomes, where cyclosporine would at most appear as graft-versus-host disease prophylaxis, not as treatment of the disease itself. The very high model score is therefore not backed by independent evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has no registered trials, a single transplantation-focused publication, and no plausible mechanistic link between calcineurin inhibition and the NADPH oxidase defect in CGD. In addition, all New Zealand licences for cyclosporine are cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Medsafe package insert warnings and contraindications (currently blocking for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Any preclinical or clinical evidence that cyclosporine itself treats CGD, rather than serving as GVHD prophylaxis in transplantation
- Clarification of the regulatory status in New Zealand, given that all licences are cancelled
- Review of other predicted indications; among the seven predictions, only brain aneurysm has some clinical literature (small 1990s studies of cyclosporine for vasospasm after subarachnoid haemorrhage), and that evidence concerns a downstream complication rather than aneurysm formation
