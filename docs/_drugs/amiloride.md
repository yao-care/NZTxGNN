## Amiloride: potassium-sparing diuretic (ENaC blocker) → malignant hypertensive renal disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Amiloride is a potassium-sparing diuretic that blocks epithelial sodium channels (ENaC). The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **malignant hypertensive renal disease**. This prediction rests on the model alone: the data compiled for this report contain **no clinical trials and no publications** on this disease.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for amiloride is not available in the data compiled for this report. The pharmacology records do show that amiloride blocks ENaC. It also acts on acid-sensing ion channels (ASIC1–3) and sodium/hydrogen exchangers. Blocking ENaC reduces renal sodium reabsorption, which lowers blood pressure.

Malignant hypertensive renal disease is kidney injury caused by severely elevated blood pressure. The prediction is plausible only through amiloride's general blood-pressure-lowering and ENaC-blocking activity. No data link amiloride to this specific condition.

Caution is needed. In this setting, rapid blood pressure control and kidney protection are the priorities. A potassium-sparing diuretic carries a risk of high blood potassium (hyperkalaemia) when kidney function is impaired. This prediction is a model output only.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information. Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available for this report, so safety screening cannot yet be completed. Hyperkalaemia in patients with impaired kidney function is the key theoretical concern for this indication.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is supported only by the TxGNN score (0.9982). No trials or publications address malignant hypertensive renal disease, and potassium retention is a concern in kidney impairment.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (Medsafe), to complete safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A literature and trial search specific to amiloride in malignant hypertension and hypertensive kidney injury
- A hyperkalaemia and renal function risk assessment for this population
