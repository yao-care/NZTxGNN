## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, pimecrolimus is a topical calcineurin inhibitor. It blocks T-cell activation and the release of inflammatory cytokines. Its efficacy in atopic dermatitis is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to seborrheic dermatitis.

Seborrheic dermatitis is a chronic, relapsing inflammatory skin condition. It involves an inflammatory response to *Malassezia* yeast in areas rich in sebaceous glands. Both atopic and seborrheic dermatitis involve T-cell-driven skin inflammation, so the same anti-inflammatory mechanism is plausible. The very high TxGNN score (0.9973) is consistent with this.

The literature supports the link. Two systematic reviews of randomized controlled trials (2012 and 2022) report that pimecrolimus 1% cream is effective and well tolerated in facial seborrheic dermatitis, with efficacy comparable to the comparators evaluated. Open-label studies (for example in Korean patients and in resistant facial disease) point the same way. The main direct randomized evidence in the trial registry is a single Phase 2 study, so the overall level is L2 rather than L1.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication signals from the knowledge graph** (these are graph-derived associations, not package-insert text): common wart, epidermodysplasia verruciformis, anogenital human papillomavirus infection, herpes simplex infection, herpes simplex dermatitis, herpes zoster, chickenpox, infectious mononucleosis, chronic Epstein-Barr virus infection syndrome, lymphatic system disease, Netherton syndrome, and ichthyosis linearis circumflexa. Most are viral skin or systemic infections, or conditions with impaired skin barrier.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were recorded, but all in the 20 shown are of unspecified severity. Examples include prednisone, triamcinolone, hydrocortisone, budesonide, omeprazole, pantoprazole, doxycycline, metformin, simvastatin and acetylsalicylic acid. Clinical significance has not been graded.
- **Long-term safety**: The literature highlights concern about a theoretical cancer risk with topical calcineurin inhibitors, and a systematic review and meta-analysis of this question exists (PMID 36370744). A 10-year paediatric registry (NCT00568997) is still active. This is relevant for any extended or off-label use.
- **Skin infection masking**: Topical immunomodulators such as pimecrolimus can alter the appearance of dermatophyte infections (tinea incognito), which can delay diagnosis.

Package insert warnings and contraindications could not be obtained for this report; please refer to the package insert for authoritative safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Seborrheic dermatitis has a direct randomized Phase 2 trial plus two systematic reviews of RCTs supporting efficacy and tolerability, with a plausible shared anti-inflammatory mechanism. However, there is no completed Phase 3 evidence, and the New Zealand licence is cancelled, so there is currently no marketed product in New Zealand.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The package insert warnings and contraindications (Medsafe), which are currently missing, so formal safety screening cannot be completed
- Detailed mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank)
- Published results and outcomes for NCT00403559
- Confirmation of a regulatory pathway and product availability in New Zealand, since the only licence is cancelled
- A long-term safety plan addressing the theoretical malignancy concern, plus guidance on use around viral skin infections and with other topical agents
