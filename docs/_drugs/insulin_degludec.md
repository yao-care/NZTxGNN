## Insulin Degludec: From Diabetes Mellitus to Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Insulin degludec (supplied in New Zealand as the degludec/insulin aspart 70/30 co-formulation, Ryzodeg) is an ultra-long-acting insulin analogue originally used for diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **type 1 diabetes mellitus**, with **40 clinical trials** and **19 publications** currently supporting this direction.
This is essentially an established, on-label use of the drug rather than true repurposing, and the only New Zealand authorization on record is cancelled.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, insulin degludec is a basal insulin analogue that binds the insulin receptor and lowers blood glucose. It has a flat, long-lasting profile, and the Ryzodeg product combines it with rapid-acting insulin aspart in a 70/30 ratio.

Type 1 diabetes is a state of absolute insulin deficiency, so insulin replacement is the cornerstone of treatment. The relationship between the original indication (diabetes mellitus) and the predicted one (type 1 diabetes) is therefore direct: type 1 diabetes is a subtype of diabetes mellitus. The very high TxGNN score agrees with this established use.

The other five predicted indications (autoimmune oophoritis, opsismodysplasia, thiamine-responsive dysfunction syndrome, classic stiff person syndrome and focal stiff limb syndrome) have no clinical trials or publications. They are computational predictions only, probably reflecting network proximity to diabetes biology (for example, shared anti-GAD autoimmunity in stiff person syndrome). They are not supported by any demonstrated mechanism and are on Hold.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 351 interactions were recorded. Most of those listed are Moderate, and a few are Minor. Moderate interactions include:
  - Other antidiabetic agents: metformin, pioglitazone, alogliptin and albiglutide (additive glucose lowering).
  - Sympathomimetics and beta-agonists: epinephrine, salbutamol, formoterol, phenylephrine and pseudoephedrine.
  - Beta-blocker: acebutolol.
  - Corticosteroids: hydrocortisone.
  - Hormonal agents: estradiol, levonorgestrel and norethisterone.
  - Others: hydrochlorothiazide, doxycycline, alpelisib and ethanol.
  - Topical corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, alclometasone) are Minor.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications. Package insert information was not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 3 randomised trials and several systematic reviews support insulin degludec in type 1 diabetes, so the evidence level is L1. However, this is an established use rather than new repurposing, and the only New Zealand authorization for the Ryzodeg product is cancelled. Safety label information and mechanism of action data are also missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Medsafe package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data, for example from DrugBank
- Confirmation of the current New Zealand regulatory pathway, since the Ryzodeg licence is cancelled
- Verification of the comparator in NCT05463744 against the registry record
- A glucose-monitoring and hypoglycaemia plan, with attention to the moderate interactions listed above
