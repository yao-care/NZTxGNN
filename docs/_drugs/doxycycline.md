# Doxycycline: From Bacterial Infections (e.g., Bronchitis, Acne) to Punctate Epithelial Keratoconjunctivitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Doxycycline is a tetracycline antibiotic originally used for bacterial infections such as bronchitis, acne, bacterial pneumonia, anthrax, gonorrhea and chlamydia-type infections.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is **punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis**, but for this indication there are **0 clinical trials** and only **1 publication** (a 1992 case report), so the evidence is very weak.
Among the other predicted indications, **chronic gingivitis** has the most literature support (about 20 publications, many on locally delivered or subantimicrobial-dose doxycycline in periodontitis), and **post-bacterial disorder** has the most trial activity (11 trials, mostly STI prophylaxis).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Doxycycline is a tetracycline-class antibiotic. It is active against *Chlamydia trachomatis* and many other bacteria, and it also has anti-inflammatory and matrix metalloproteinase (MMP)-inhibiting effects, which are relevant to the ocular surface.

The link to punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis is indirect. The only publication is a 1992 report of two patients who developed persistent punctate keratitis after chlamydial follicular conjunctivitis had been treated with tetracycline or doxycycline. It does not test doxycycline as a treatment for this condition. The prediction should therefore be seen as a hypothesis, not as supported efficacy.

For context, the other predicted indications differ in strength:
- **Chronic gingivitis** (L2): MMP inhibition and antibacterial activity against periodontal pathogens are supported by many studies of local and subantimicrobial-dose doxycycline as an adjunct to scaling and root planing. However, most of this work is in periodontitis, not gingivitis alone.
- **Post-bacterial disorder** (L1 as scored): the trials are mainly prophylaxis against new STIs, so the match to a post-infectious disorder is indirect.
- **Others** (otitis externa, postinfectious vasculitis, infective urethral stricture, Chagas cardiomyopathy, exposure keratitis): evidence is limited to veterinary studies, case reports or negative preclinical data. For infection-related hemolytic uremic syndrome there is no evidence at all, and antibiotics are generally avoided in STEC-associated HUS.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (knowledge-graph associations, not label text)**: *Clostridium difficile* colitis, perinatal necrotizing enterocolitis, and dyskinesia of the oesophagus.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified; the 20 reviewed include these main ones:
  - **Major**: isotretinoin, acitretin, aminolevulinic acid (systemic).
  - **Moderate**: aluminium hydroxide, aminophylline, amobarbital, amoxicillin, ampicillin, anisindione, atracurium, attapulgite, balsalazide, bedaquiline, and others.
  - **Minor**: acetazolamide, amiloride, bendroflumethiazide.
- **Other indication-specific concerns**: tetracyclines showed cytotoxicity in human tympanic membrane fibroblasts in vitro, a concern for topical ear use. Resistance (notably in gonorrhea and *Staphylococcus aureus*) is a concern for prophylaxis use.

Package insert warnings and contraindications are not available in the data compiled for this report; please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked prediction, punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis, is supported only by a single 1992 case report with no clinical trials (L4), and doxycycline has no currently valid New Zealand licence. The most promising alternative, chronic gingivitis (L2, "Proceed with Guardrails" in the underlying scoring), has a stronger evidence base, but it is mostly from periodontitis studies and needs confirmation for gingivitis itself.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indications, which are blocking for safety screening.
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data.
- For punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis: controlled studies of doxycycline in ocular surface disease, and a decision on route (oral versus topical ocular).
- For chronic gingivitis: confirmation that the periodontitis evidence applies to gingivitis, with use as an adjunct to mechanical therapy and local or subantimicrobial dosing to limit resistance.
- A decision on which predicted indication to prioritise, and whether a New Zealand licence would need to be re-established.
