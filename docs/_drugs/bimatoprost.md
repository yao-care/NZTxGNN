## Bimatoprost: From Open-Angle Glaucoma to Alopecia
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Bimatoprost is a prostamide (prostaglandin F2-alpha analogue) originally used for open-angle glaucoma and ocular hypertension, and it is also used for eyelash hypotrichosis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **alopecia**. This is the best-supported prediction among the top-ranked candidates, with **11 clinical trials** (mostly Phase 1/2) and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.
Although the TxGNN score is highest for other diseases, those higher-ranked predictions have no supporting evidence, so this report focuses on alopecia as the most evidence-backed candidate.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available. Based on known information, bimatoprost is a prostamide F2-alpha analogue that acts on FP-like receptors. Its efficacy in glaucoma is established, and it is also used to treat eyelash hypotrichosis. It prolongs the anagen (growth) phase of the hair cycle and stimulates hair follicles.

The link between the original and new indications comes from a well-known side effect: patients using bimatoprost eye drops grew longer, thicker and darker eyelashes. This led to the approved use for eyelash hypotrichosis and to investigation in scalp, eyebrow and other forms of hair loss. Mechanistically, follicle stimulation and anagen prolongation may apply to androgenetic alopecia and some other hair-loss conditions.

There are important limits. Efficacy for scalp hair appears modest, and the Phase 2 trials in androgenetic alopecia did not lead to an approved scalp indication. In autoimmune alopecia areata, bimatoprost does not address the immune attack on follicles. The eyelash hypotrichosis use is also largely covered already, so the new value lies mainly in scalp and other hair-loss indications.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions:** 66 potential interactions were identified, all with an unspecified severity level. Examples include calcitriol, phentermine, pantoprazole, morphine, metformin, omeprazole, prednisone, simvastatin and ondansetron. Their clinical significance is not graded.

Please refer to the package insert for label warnings and contraindications, as these were not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Alopecia has the best support among the predictions, with multiple completed Phase 2 trials in androgenetic alopecia, but none are Phase 3 and none led to an approved scalp indication, and efficacy appears modest. The market status is cancelled only, label safety data are missing, and the other top-ranked predictions have no credible support, so alopecia should be treated as a research question rather than a candidate to proceed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Obtain the Medsafe package insert to complete warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Obtain mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Review the results of the Phase 2 androgenetic alopecia trials (NCT01325337, NCT01325350, NCT01904721) to confirm efficacy against minoxidil
- Define the target alopecia subtype and a scalp-appropriate formulation and route, since only an ophthalmic form is on record
- Clarify New Zealand regulatory status given the cancelled licence
