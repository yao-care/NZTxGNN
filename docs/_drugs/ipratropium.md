## Ipratropium: Nasal cavity disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Ipratropium is an inhaled and intranasal anticholinergic (muscarinic antagonist) originally used for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), rhinitis, bronchitis and emphysema.
This prediction largely confirms an existing use rather than a true new indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Pharmacology records show that ipratropium binds muscarinic receptors M1 to M5 (CHRM1–CHRM5). It is a non-selective muscarinic antagonist, and its recorded clinical use is bronchospasm associated with COPD.

Blocking M3 receptors on airway smooth muscle reduces vagally mediated bronchoconstriction, which is the core physiological problem in obstructive lung disease. The predicted indication overlaps heavily with the drug's original uses (COPD, bronchitis, emphysema), so this is mostly confirmation of an established use rather than true repurposing.

Lower-ranked predictions (nasal cavity disease, pharyngitis, tracheal disease, anaphylaxis and others) have much weaker support. Most have only indirect or preclinical evidence, or none at all, and are not recommended for advancement.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease associations flagged as contraindications in the knowledge graph** (not taken from label text): hypertension, long QT syndrome, epilepsy, potassium deficiency, diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis, benign prostatic hyperplasia, bladder neck obstruction, ocular hypertension, angle-closure glaucoma, acidosis, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease, hyperthyroidism/Graves disease/thyrotoxicosis, myocardial ischemia and coronary artery disease. These need verification against the current package insert.
- **Drug interactions**: The interaction query returned only receptor-binding records (muscarinic M1–M5), with no drug–drug interaction entries.
- **Other signals from the literature**: A case report describes severe anaphylaxis after ipratropium inhalation, and another describes unilateral mydriasis after inhalation in an infant.

Package insert warnings and contraindications could not be retrieved, so please refer to the package insert for authoritative safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
- Obstructive lung disease is essentially an established use of ipratropium. A large completed Phase 3 trial and extensive review literature support it, but the indication is not new.
- The other predicted indications are not recommended to advance. Nasal cavity disease and respiratory malformation are research questions at most. Pharyngitis, acute laryngopharyngitis, papillary conjunctivitis, Rienhoff syndrome, tracheal disease, anaphylaxis and food-dependent exercise-induced anaphylaxis are on Hold.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Medsafe package insert warnings and contraindications, which block safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A review of the knowledge-graph contraindications (cardiovascular, glaucoma, urinary retention and others) against label text
- Confirmation of which formulation (nebulised, inhaler or nasal) is intended for any new use
