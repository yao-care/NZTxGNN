## One-Sentence Summary

Acitretin is an oral second-generation retinoid (a vitamin A derivative) that is already marketed in New Zealand. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **acne** (score 0.9994). Support is limited: **1 clinical trial** and **18 publications** were compiled, but the only trial and most of the studies address hidradenitis suppurativa, a different follicular disease. Only **one case report** deals directly with acne (nodulocystic acne), so the evidence level is **L4**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this report. Based on general pharmacology, acitretin is a systemic retinoid. It modulates keratinocyte differentiation and proliferation. It is also thought to reduce sebaceous gland activity and follicular hyperkeratinisation, both of which are central to acne.

The clinical signal comes mainly from hidradenitis suppurativa (also called acne inversa). This is a related follicular occlusion disorder, not acne vulgaris. For acne vulgaris itself, isotretinoin is the preferred retinoid, and the literature on acitretin is thin. A single case report (PMID 12080949) describes acitretin in a patient with severe nodulocystic acne and hidradenitis suppurativa who had improved on isotretinoin but had persistent lesions.

The mechanistic rationale is plausible, but the direct clinical evidence is very limited. Acitretin's teratogenicity and very long effective washout period are major constraints.

## Safety Considerations

- **Key Warnings**:
  - Acitretin is a known human teratogen. Its very long effective washout period (conversion to etretinate in the presence of alcohol) is a major constraint, especially for women of childbearing potential.
  - Hepatotoxicity and bone effects are recognised concerns for retinoids and need monitoring.
- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen returned 100 records.
  - Major: tetracyclines (doxycycline, minocycline, tetracycline) and vitamin A.
  - Moderate: sulfonylureas (acetohexamide, chlorpropamide, glimepiride, glipizide, glyburide, tolazamide, tolbutamide) and naltrexone.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The TxGNN score is very high (0.9994), but direct evidence for acne is limited to a single case report (evidence level L4).
- The only trial and most of the literature concern hidradenitis suppurativa, a different disease.
- Isotretinoin is already the preferred retinoid for acne, and acitretin's teratogenicity is a serious risk.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Controlled studies of acitretin in acne vulgaris or nodulocystic acne, or results from the hidradenitis suppurativa trial.
- A comparison against isotretinoin.
- Detailed mechanism of action data.
- A pregnancy-prevention and monitoring plan, based on the package insert warnings and contraindications.
