# Arsenic Trioxide: From Acute Promyelocytic Leukaemia to Myelodysplastic Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Arsenic trioxide was originally used to treat acute promyelocytic leukaemia and related myeloid leukaemias.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is unclassified myelodysplastic syndrome, which has no direct supporting studies. The best-supported prediction is **myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS)**, with **19 clinical trials** and **20 publications** linked to it, though none of these is a Phase 3 trial with results.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available from the compiled data. Based on the published literature, arsenic trioxide is an established antileukaemic agent that induces apoptosis, promotes cell differentiation and has antiproliferative effects. Its efficacy in acute promyelocytic leukaemia is well recognised, and reviews describe cure rates of 80–90% when it is combined with all-trans retinoic acid.

The top-ranked prediction, unclassified MDS, has no linked trials or literature. The only indirect support is arsenic trioxide's activity in the broader MDS category, and the very high score is a computational result only.

The broader MDS entry is more credible. MDS is a clonal myeloid disorder that can progress to acute myeloid leukaemia, so it shares biology with the original indications. Laboratory work points to NF-κB and FLIP modulation, effects on BCL2-family genes and proposed TP53 reactivation. The drug has been tested alone and with ascorbic acid, thalidomide, decitabine, azacitidine, cytarabine and retinoids. A 2023 systematic review and network meta-analysis suggests some activity, but trials were small and conflicting, and many ended early.

Other predictions in the list have limited or indirect support:
- **Aregenerative anaemia (aplastic anaemia)**: several small clinical series in refractory severe aplastic anaemia, with immunomodulatory effects reported. One small phase 2 study was stopped at eight weeks because of non-response.
- **Ewing sarcoma**: mostly preclinical data, including Hedgehog/GLI inhibition and potentiation of etoposide.
- **Refractory cytopenia of childhood, congenital sideroblastic anaemia, del(5q), dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans, liposarcoma and ovarian myxoid liposarcoma**: little or no direct evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-based contraindications (knowledge graph, not label text)**: long QT syndrome, familial long QT syndrome, torsades de pointes, atrioventricular block, potassium deficiency, kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease and pyoureter.
- **Drug Interactions** (100 interactions recorded; examples):
  - *Major*: hydrocortisone, amphotericin B (including lipid complex, cholesteryl sulfate and liposomal forms), cisapride, clarithromycin, dolasetron, granisetron, levofloxacin. Most of these are QT-prolonging or electrolyte-depleting.
  - *Moderate*: famotidine, loperamide, bisacodyl, castor oil, glycerin, lactitol, lactulose, magnesium citrate, magnesium hydroxide.
  - *Minor*: metronidazole.

Please also refer to the package insert, as label warnings and contraindications were not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (unclassified MDS and the other low-evidence predictions); Proceed with Guardrails (myelodysplastic syndrome)**

**Rationale:**
- The top-ranked prediction, unclassified MDS, has no trials or literature behind it, so it cannot be advanced on the model score alone.
- For MDS, many phase 1/2 trials and a 2023 systematic review show some activity, but trials were small, often terminated early, and no phase 3 RCT demonstrates benefit. The drug is also cancelled-only in New Zealand.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Medsafe package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A confirmatory randomised trial in MDS, ideally in TP53-mutated or low-risk subgroups where oral arsenic studies are under way
- A cardiac safety plan (baseline and serial ECG, electrolyte correction, avoidance of QT-prolonging co-medications)
- A regulatory or supply pathway, since no product is currently authorised in New Zealand
- Any exploration of aplastic anaemia or Ewing sarcoma should be framed as a research question, given the limited and mostly indirect evidence
