# Caffeine: From Headache and Rhinitis Conditions to Nasal Cavity Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Caffeine is originally associated with allergic and vasomotor rhinitis, migraine, cluster headache and related headache disorders, as well as pharyngitis and the common cold.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **nasal cavity disease**, but there are currently **0 clinical trials** and only **3 publications** (a review, a preclinical formulation study and an off-topic animal study), none showing benefit in nasal disease.
This is a model prediction with very limited supporting evidence.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Caffeine is an adenosine receptor antagonist (A1, A2A, A2B and A3) and also acts on intracellular calcium-release channels (IP3R1 and ryanodine receptors RyR1–3). Detailed mechanism-of-action data beyond this receptor pharmacology are not available.

The predicted indication is related to the original indications of allergic and vasomotor rhinitis, which are nasal conditions. Bitter taste receptors are also expressed in airway and nasal tissue and may mediate responses to caffeine, and a nasal caffeine gel has been studied as a delivery route for cognition after sleep deprivation.

However, none of this shows therapeutic benefit in nasal disease itself. Rhinitis as an original indication does not establish efficacy for the broad "nasal cavity disease" category, so the prediction should be treated as a hypothesis.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but there are no clinical trials and the literature shows no benefit of caffeine in nasal cavity disease. The only New Zealand licence is cancelled, and label-based safety data were not available.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A more specific nasal indication (for example, allergic or vasomotor rhinitis) with a defined mechanism and route
- Controlled clinical or well-designed preclinical evidence in nasal disease
- Medsafe package insert warnings and contraindications
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Assessment of route and formulation, since the only New Zealand licence is an oral liquid and a nasal product would be a new formulation
