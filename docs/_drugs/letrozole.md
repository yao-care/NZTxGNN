# Letrozole: From an Unspecified Original Indication to Female Breast Carcinoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Letrozole is a nonsteroidal aromatase inhibitor. The New Zealand licence records compiled for this report do not state an approved indication.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **female breast carcinoma**, with **39 clinical trials** and **20 publications** listed for this prediction.
This is an established use in hormone-receptor-positive breast cancer, not a new repurposing finding.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known pharmacology, letrozole is a nonsteroidal aromatase inhibitor. It blocks the conversion of androgens to estrogens, which lowers the estrogen levels that drive hormone-receptor-positive breast cancer.

Large Phase 3 programmes support this use. MONALEESA-2, PALOMA-2 and COMPLEEMENT-1 tested letrozole alone or combined with CDK4/6 inhibitors in advanced HR+/HER2- breast cancer. Because letrozole is already standard care for this disease, the high TxGNN score reflects a known indication.

**Other predicted indications:**
- **Estrogen-receptor positive breast cancer** (Evidence Level L1, Proceed with Guardrails): the same established use, supported by multiple Phase 3 RCTs and meta-analyses.
- **Hormone-resistant breast carcinoma** (L2, Research Question): evidence is for combination strategies, not letrozole alone.
- **Bilateral breast carcinoma** and **breast carcinoma by gene expression profile** (L3, Research Question): the trials are general breast cancer or biomarker studies, not designed for these entities.
- **Nipple carcinoma** (L4, Hold): only case reports exist.
- **Estrogen-receptor negative breast cancer** (L4, Hold): likely a mislabel. An aromatase inhibitor is not expected to work in ER-negative disease, and the matched trials are in ER+ populations.
- **Ehrlich tumor carcinoma** (L5, Hold): a murine model with only a nanoparticle delivery study.
- **Breast fibrocystic disease** and **benign mammary dysplasia** (L5, Hold): no trials or literature, and the risks of estrogen deprivation are hard to justify in benign conditions.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (from a drug-disease knowledge graph, not label text):** liver failure, cirrhosis of liver, osteoporosis, postmenopausal osteoporosis, hyperbilirubinemia, hereditary hyperbilirubinemia, Gilbert syndrome, cerebrovascular disorder.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were found. The only one graded is **aprepitant (Moderate)**. Others, such as calcitriol, glimepiride, metformin, simvastatin, omeprazole, pantoprazole, lansoprazole, famotidine, prednisone, aspirin and morphine, are listed without a severity grade.
- **Literature signals:** case reports describe letrozole-related maculopathy and tumour flare with tumour lysis syndrome. Bone-related adverse effects are also noted in the literature.

Please refer to the package insert for label warnings; none were available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 3 RCTs support letrozole in HR+ breast cancer, so the evidence level is L1. This is an established standard of care rather than a novel repurposing finding. Both New Zealand licences are cancelled, and the safety and mechanism data are incomplete.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The Medsafe package insert, with warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Clarification of the current New Zealand regulatory status, since both licences are cancelled and no approved indication is recorded
- A monitoring plan for bone density and liver function
- Drop or de-prioritise the ER-negative, Ehrlich tumour, fibrocystic disease and benign mammary dysplasia predictions
