## Mefenamic Acid: Headache disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Mefenamic acid is a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) that inhibits prostaglandin synthesis and is marketed in New Zealand as Ponstan capsules.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **headache disorder**.
No clinical trials are registered for this prediction, but **19 publications** exist, including several double-blind randomised trials. These studied migraine, which is one subtype of headache disorder.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Mefenamic acid is known to be an NSAID that blocks prostaglandin synthesis. Prostaglandins contribute to headache pain and to neurogenic inflammation, the inflammatory signalling around pain-sensing nerves, so a mechanistic fit with headache is plausible.

The strongest support comes from migraine, a major headache disorder. Double-blind trials report benefit in acute attacks (compared with paracetamol), in menstrual migraine (compared with placebo), and in migraine prevention (compared with propranolol and placebo). This evidence is specific to migraine and does not show that mefenamic acid works across headache disorders in general. The trials are old and small, and other NSAIDs have stronger guideline support.

## Safety Considerations

- **Key Warnings (from the literature):**
  - Prolonged use has been linked to severe enteropathy with villous atrophy.
  - NSAID overuse is associated with medication-overuse headache.
  - Analgesic overuse in chronic headache patients has been linked to chronic kidney disease, and mefenamic acid was the most commonly used NSAID in one such study.
- **Drug Interactions:** The interaction screen returned 100 records. Examples rated Moderate include:
  - corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, triamcinolone)
  - acetylsalicylic acid
  - metformin
  - chlorpropamide
  - mesalazine and balsalazide
  - aprepitant
  - potassium citrate and potassium bicarbonate
  - bowel-preparation agents (picosulfuric acid, polyethylene glycol with electrolytes)

  Examples rated Minor include famotidine, ranitidine, cimetidine, dapagliflozin and linaclotide.

Please refer to the package insert for the full warnings and contraindications, which were not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The supporting evidence is limited to small, older double-blind trials in migraine, which is only one subtype of the predicted disease. Other NSAIDs have stronger guideline support. Package-insert safety information has not yet been reviewed, and there are known risks with prolonged use (enteropathy, medication overuse, renal effects).

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the Medsafe package insert for warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Clarification of whether the efficacy evidence extends beyond migraine to other headache disorders, ideally with newer, adequately powered controlled trials
- A comparison against better-supported NSAIDs, and a plan to limit treatment duration and monitor gastrointestinal and renal safety
