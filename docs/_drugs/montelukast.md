## Montelukast: Bronchitis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Montelukast is a cysteinyl leukotriene receptor antagonist, marketed in New Zealand as a single-ingredient tablet.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **bronchitis**, with a prediction score of 0.9995.
The data compiled for this report include **17 clinical trials** and **20 publications**. Evidence is **Level L2**, based on small controlled trials in non-asthmatic eosinophilic bronchitis and recurrent obstructive bronchitis. Many of the other trials studied the related condition bronchiolitis, or post-transplant bronchiolitis obliterans, rather than bronchitis itself.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. The literature describes montelukast as a cysteinyl leukotriene receptor antagonist. Cysteinyl leukotrienes are lipid mediators that drive airway inflammation, and they are released during respiratory viral infection (PMID 24118637).

Airway inflammation involving eosinophils and leukotrienes is also described in some forms of bronchitis. The clinical studies are consistent with this idea. A placebo-controlled study tested montelukast added to inhaled budesonide in non-asthmatic eosinophilic bronchitis (NCT01121016, PMID 25563311), and a Phase 4 study tested it in children with recurrent obstructive bronchitis (NCT04613180).

Bronchitis is a broad term, and the evidence differs by subtype. The data for infant bronchiolitis, a related but distinct condition, are mixed, and the Cochrane review in that area is about leukotriene inhibitors in infants and young children. Benefit in one form of bronchitis should not be assumed to carry over to others.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is high (0.9995). The best-matched evidence is small controlled trials in non-asthmatic eosinophilic bronchitis and recurrent obstructive bronchitis, which supports Level L2. Much of the remaining data concerns related conditions such as bronchiolitis, so any further work should stay within defined bronchitis subtypes.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Published results from NCT01121016 and NCT04613180, and a formal review of their quality
- Clarification of which bronchitis subtype (for example eosinophilic, recurrent childhood) is the target population
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the marketed products
- Mechanism-of-action data specific to bronchitis
- A review of the moderate-severity interactions, particularly for patients taking several medicines
