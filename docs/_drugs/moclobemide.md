# Moclobemide: From Anxiety Disorder to Agoraphobia

## One-Sentence Summary

Moclobemide is a reversible inhibitor of monoamine oxidase A, originally used for anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder and dysthymic disorder.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **agoraphobia**,
with **no registered clinical trials** but **10 publications** (including 2 randomised controlled trials in panic disorder) currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, moclobemide is a reversible inhibitor of monoamine oxidase A (RIMA). Database records also show it acting on both MAO-A and MAO-B. Inhibiting these enzymes raises serotonin, noradrenaline and dopamine levels. This mechanistic explanation comes from general knowledge of the drug class, not from the supplied data.

Agoraphobia is usually treated as part of panic disorder with agoraphobia, which sits close to the drug's original indications (anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder). The repurposing distance is therefore short. The literature includes randomised trials of moclobemide in panic disorder with or without agoraphobia, which supports the prediction.

The evidence is indirect, however. The trials studied panic disorder with agoraphobia as a combined condition, not agoraphobia on its own.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: Pharmacology records show moclobemide acts on monoamine oxidase A (MAOA) and monoamine oxidase B (MAOB). No drug–drug interaction entries with severity levels are available.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence is moderate (L2), with two randomised trials in panic disorder with agoraphobia and a plausible mechanism. However, there are no registered clinical trials for agoraphobia itself. Moclobemide currently has no valid New Zealand authorisation (both licences are cancelled). Package insert safety information has not been obtained, so safety screening cannot proceed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Medsafe package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of the regulatory pathway, since there is no currently valid New Zealand authorisation
- Review of the two RCTs to confirm effect sizes specific to agoraphobia symptoms
- Safety and interaction assessment, particularly with serotonergic agents

*Note: The second predicted indication, benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy (TxGNN score 99.30%), has no supporting trials or literature and no supported mechanistic link. It is not recommended for further evaluation at this time (Hold).*
