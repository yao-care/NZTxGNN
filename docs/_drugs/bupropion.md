## Bupropion: From Depression and Smoking Cessation to Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Bupropion was originally used to treat depression and to help people stop smoking (nicotine dependence).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)**,
with **6 clinical trials** (including one completed Phase 3 randomized, placebo-controlled trial) and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacological knowledge, bupropion inhibits the reuptake of norepinephrine and dopamine (the pharmacology data lists the norepinephrine transporter NET and the dopamine transporter DAT as targets). It has no serotonergic activity.

Established ADHD treatments such as methylphenidate and atomoxetine also act on the dopamine and norepinephrine systems. This overlap gives bupropion biological plausibility in ADHD. Its efficacy in depression and nicotine dependence is already established, and ADHD frequently co-occurs with both conditions.

Several reviews list bupropion as a second-line or non-stimulant option for adult ADHD, mainly for patients who do not respond to or cannot tolerate stimulants.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (these are drug-disease links from the TxGNN knowledge graph, not package insert text): epilepsy, anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, bipolar mania, psychotic disorders, substance abuse/dependence, opiate dependence, hypertension, liver disease or failure, cirrhosis, kidney disease, myocardial infarction, and central nervous system neoplasm.
- **Relevance to the ADHD population:** many candidate patients have comorbid substance use disorder, anxiety or psychiatric conditions, which overlap with several of these flagged conditions. Seizure risk and cardiovascular effects deserve particular attention.
- **Pharmacological targets:** bupropion acts on NET and DAT, consistent with its dopamine and norepinephrine reuptake inhibition.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available for this report. Please refer to the package insert for authoritative safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
One completed Phase 3 randomized, placebo-controlled trial, a Cochrane review, and several network meta-analyses support bupropion in adult ADHD, and its dopamine/norepinephrine mechanism is plausible. However, only one such Phase 3 trial is confirmed, bupropion is not a first-line ADHD agent, it has no valid New Zealand licence, and package insert safety data are missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Medsafe package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking gap for safety screening)
- Confirmed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Review of the full results of NCT00048360 and the Cochrane review's conclusions on effect size and certainty
- Clarification of the study drug and design in NCT00061087
- A regulatory pathway plan, given the cancelled New Zealand licence
- A safety screening plan for seizure risk, cardiovascular effects, and psychiatric and substance-use comorbidity

**Other predicted indications (for reference):** ADHD, inattentive type is a subtype of the lead indication with only indirect evidence (research question stage). The remaining predictions (faciodigitogenital syndrome, specific developmental disorder, chondromyxoid fibroma, proximal 16p11.2 microdeletion syndrome, hypervitaminosis, benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy, trigeminal nerve neoplasm, and an obsolete hypertelorism term) have no supporting clinical trials or relevant literature and should be held.
