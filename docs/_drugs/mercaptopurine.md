## Mercaptopurine: Myeloid leukemia
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Mercaptopurine is a purine antimetabolite used in leukaemia chemotherapy. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **myeloid leukaemia (myeloid leukemia)**, with an evidence level of **L3**. Support comes from **15 listed clinical trials**, most of them multi-drug regimens in which mercaptopurine's own contribution cannot be separated, and from cohort and pilot studies of oral mercaptopurine maintenance in acute myeloid leukaemia.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacology, mercaptopurine is a purine antimetabolite. Its active thioguanine nucleotides are incorporated into DNA and RNA and inhibit de novo purine synthesis. This is a plausible way to act on rapidly dividing myeloid blasts.

Evidence in myeloid disease comes almost entirely from combination regimens. Examples are oral mercaptopurine plus methotrexate maintenance in AML patients ineligible for transplantation, ALL-like consolidation in childhood AML, and a risk-stratified protocol for AML in Down syndrome. Because mercaptopurine is always given with other drugs, its independent effect cannot be isolated.

## Safety Considerations

- **Conditions of concern (listed as contraindications in the data compiled for this report, not label text)**: kidney disease, liver disease, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, leukopenia, agranulocytosis, anaemia, hyperbilirubinaemia, Gilbert syndrome, cholestasis, and hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma.
- **Drug Interactions** (100 interactions recorded; main ones shown):
  - Moderate: mesalazine, balsalazide, olsalazine, sulfasalazine, naltrexone
  - Minor: levofloxacin
- Genetic variation in thiopurine metabolism (such as NUDT15) is linked to severe bone marrow suppression.

Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Mercaptopurine has a plausible mechanism in myeloid blasts. Support is limited to a cohort study and a prospective pilot study of oral maintenance in AML, plus multi-drug trials that do not isolate its effect. No randomised trial tests mercaptopurine itself in myeloid leukaemia, so the evidence level is L3.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Medsafe
- A controlled study that isolates mercaptopurine's contribution in AML
- Haematological monitoring and pharmacogenetic (TPMT/NUDT15) testing plans
