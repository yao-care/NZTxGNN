## One-Sentence Summary

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Axitinib is known as a VEGFR-inhibiting kinase inhibitor, so its main effect is to cut off the blood supply that tumours need to grow.

Many soft-tissue sarcomas, including liposarcoma, depend on new blood vessel formation. An anti-angiogenic drug is therefore a plausible fit. A 2016 laboratory study supports this. It found that axitinib had anti-angiogenic and antitumour activity in myxoid liposarcoma models, which is consistent with its VEGFR-inhibiting mechanism.

This remains a mechanistic and preclinical rationale only. No human efficacy data are available for liposarcoma.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions:** The interaction screening returned 100 records. The first 20 reviewed include:
- **Major:** clarithromycin and dexamethasone.
- **Moderate:** aprepitant, clotrimazole, miconazole and naltrexone.
- **Minor:** acid-reducing agents and antacids, such as famotidine, ranitidine, cimetidine, nizatidine, the proton pump inhibitors (rabeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, dexlansoprazole), and aluminium, calcium and magnesium salts.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only supporting evidence is one preclinical study in a myxoid liposarcoma subtype, and no clinical trials exist. The high model score and the plausible anti-angiogenic mechanism justify further research, but not clinical adoption yet.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clinical data (at least an early-phase trial) in liposarcoma patients
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- The package insert warnings and contraindications from Medsafe, for a safety review
- A review of the interactions with strong enzyme-affecting drugs (clarithromycin, dexamethasone) for the intended patient population
