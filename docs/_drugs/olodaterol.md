## Olodaterol: From Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease to Bronchitis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Olodaterol is a once-daily long-acting β2-agonist (LABA) originally used to treat chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **bronchitis**, but this is supported by **0 clinical trials** and only **2 publications**, both focused on COPD rather than bronchitis itself.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on the established pharmacology of the drug class, olodaterol is a long-acting β2-agonist. It activates β2-adrenoceptors, raises intracellular cAMP and relaxes airway smooth muscle, producing sustained bronchodilation.

Chronic bronchitis is a recognised phenotype of COPD, the drug's original indication. The very high TxGNN score (0.9984) therefore largely reflects overlap with the existing COPD indication rather than a novel repurposing signal. Acute bronchitis would not be expected to benefit from a LABA in the same way, because bronchodilation is not the main treatment goal there.

In short, the prediction is plausible for chronic bronchitis within COPD. It is not a strong signal for a genuinely new use.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

Currently no related clinical trials registered for bronchitis.

*Note:* Many trials exist for the closely related category "obstructive lung disease" (mainly COPD, including several completed Phase 3 studies of olodaterol alone and with tiotropium). These support the existing COPD use rather than bronchitis as a separate indication.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 326 known interactions are recorded, all of the ones listed here at moderate level. Examples include:
  - Sympathomimetics: epinephrine (including ophthalmic and topical forms), ephedrine, phentermine, diethylpropion and fenfluramine
  - QT-relevant agents: clarithromycin, cisapride, dolasetron, palonosetron and granisetron
  - Others: famotidine, loperamide, canagliflozin, ertugliflozin, semaglutide and albiglutide
- **Class consideration**: LABA monotherapy is not recommended in asthma because of safety concerns. Concomitant beta-blockers, QT-prolonging agents and other sympathomimetics should be reviewed.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
No clinical trials and only two COPD-focused publications support bronchitis. The high prediction score mostly reflects overlap with the approved COPD indication. The only New Zealand authorization has been cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clarify whether the target is chronic bronchitis (already covered by COPD) or acute bronchitis (little mechanistic rationale)
- Evidence directly evaluating olodaterol in bronchitis
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Medsafe
- Confirmation of current product availability in New Zealand
- A review plan for drug interactions, particularly beta-blockers, QT-prolonging agents and sympathomimetics
