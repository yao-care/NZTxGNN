## Diclofenac: From Arthritis and Actinic Keratosis to Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Diclofenac is a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) originally used for rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis, ankylosing spondylitis and actinic keratosis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis (JIA)**, the highest-ranked prediction that has any supporting evidence,
with **0 registered clinical trials** and **14 publications** currently supporting this direction. This is mostly an extension within the NSAID-for-arthritis class.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for diclofenac were not available in the data compiled for this report. Known pharmacology data list its targets as COX-1 (PTGS1) and COX-2 (PTGS2), with additional interactions at TRPM3, PPARγ, ASIC3 and the amino acid transporter SLC36A1. Diclofenac is a non-selective COX inhibitor that reduces prostaglandin-mediated inflammation, pain and stiffness.

The same mechanism underlies its established use in rheumatoid arthritis and osteoarthritis. JIA shares the inflammatory synovitis phenotype of these conditions, so the prediction is mechanistically coherent. However, it is largely an extension within the NSAID-for-arthritis class rather than a true repurposing to a new mechanism.

By contrast, the other high-scoring predictions (genetic skeletal dysplasias, hereditary hair disorders, WHIM syndrome, alopecia areata) lack any plausible link to COX inhibition and appear to be driven by network proximity in the model.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Diclofenac's COX-inhibitory mechanism is consistent with use in JIA, and several small, older clinical studies and reviews support it. However, there are no registered trials, the evidence is dated and low-grade (L3), and there is no valid New Zealand licence. The other top-ranked predictions are model-only (L5) and should be held.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Medsafe package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Modern, controlled paediatric efficacy and safety data, including dosing in children
- Review of whether a diclofenac product can be re-licensed or supplied in New Zealand, and of cardiovascular, gastrointestinal, hepatic and renal safety in children
- Comparison against current JIA standard of care (e.g. other NSAIDs and disease-modifying agents)
