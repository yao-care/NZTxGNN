# Chlorambucil: From Hodgkin Lymphoma and Other Lymphoid Malignancies to Pregerminal Center Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia/Small Lymphocytic Lymphoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Chlorambucil is an alkylating agent originally used for Hodgkin lymphoma, follicular lymphoma, mature B-cell neoplasms and acute lymphoblastic leukemia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **pregerminal center chronic lymphocytic leukemia/small lymphocytic lymphoma (CLL/SLL)**,
but there are currently **no registered clinical trials** and only **1 publication** (a 2003 review), so the evidence is indirect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, chlorambucil is a nitrogen mustard alkylating agent that cross-links DNA and has long been used in B-cell lymphoid malignancies. Its efficacy in several of these diseases is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to this CLL/SLL subtype.

CLL is not among the listed original indications, but mature B-cell neoplasm is, and CLL/SLL falls within that disease family. The prediction is therefore consistent with the drug's known biology. The high score is a model output, however, and is not clinical evidence for this particular subtype.

The same model also ranks closely related candidates: mutated-IGHV CLL/SLL (rank 2, same score, evidence from prediction only), primary pulmonary lymphoma (rank 4, L3) and small cell lung carcinoma (rank 8, L3). Their evidence is summarised below where relevant.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: epilepsy; nephrotic syndrome (including familial nephrotic syndrome and lipoid nephrosis); thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, leukopenia, lymphopenia, anemia (including macrocytic anemia); interstitial lung disease, pulmonary fibrosis; drug-induced liver injury; acute leukemia (including mixed phenotype and undifferentiated forms).
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found in total. Examples of the main ones:
  - Major: deferiprone, samarium (153Sm) lexidronam
  - Moderate: amphotericin B (including lipid, cholesteryl sulfate and liposomal forms), palifermin, strontium chloride Sr-89, mercaptopurine, roflumilast, chloramphenicol
  - Minor: levofloxacin
- **Safety signals in the literature**: Hodgkin disease cohorts report raised risks of second malignancies, including lung cancer and acute leukemia, after treatment that included alkylating agents. A Phase I trial reported dose-limiting central nervous system toxicity at high doses.

Package insert warnings and contraindications for the New Zealand product were not available and need to be obtained.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is biologically plausible, since chlorambucil is an established alkylator in B-cell malignancies. The only supporting item is a 2003 review with no trials. The only New Zealand licence has been cancelled, and a head-to-head Phase 3 comparison (ibrutinib versus chlorambucil, RESONATE-2, in the literature set for another candidate) shows that newer targeted agents are the comparator standard in CLL/SLL.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data
- The Medsafe package insert, to complete safety screening of warnings and contraindications
- Subtype-specific clinical evidence (trials or cohorts) for chlorambucil in pregerminal center CLL/SLL, compared against current standard therapy
- Confirmation of the current regulatory and supply status in New Zealand, given the cancelled licence

**Other candidates (for reference):**
- Primary pulmonary lymphoma (L3, Research Question): evidence is limited to case reports, a cohort study, guidelines and a non-specific Phase II study.
- Small cell lung carcinoma (L3, Research Question): the studies are old, small and indirect, and predate current standard care.
- Mutated-IGHV CLL/SLL, ALK-positive large B-cell lymphoma, well-differentiated fetal adenocarcinoma of the lung, pulmonary blastoma (L5, Hold): prediction only.
- Acute lymphoblastic leukemia (L4, Hold): already a listed original indication, so not a true repurposing candidate.
