## Daunorubicin: From Acute Leukaemia to Hodgkin's Lymphoma
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Daunorubicin is an anthracycline chemotherapy drug, originally used to treat acute lymphoblastic and acute myeloid leukaemia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Hodgkin's lymphoma**,
but the support is limited: **2 Phase 2 clinical trials** (both multi-drug regimens, one in non-Hodgkin lymphoma) and **19 publications**, mostly reviews, older studies and non-Hodgkin lymphoma work.

Neither isolates daunorubicin's effect nor addresses Hodgkin's lymphoma directly. By the strict criteria (a completed Phase 2/3 RCT), the evidence is closer to L3–L4.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, daunorubicin is an anthracycline that intercalates into DNA and inhibits topoisomerase II. Its efficacy in acute leukaemia is well established.

The link to Hodgkin's lymphoma is mainly through drug class. Doxorubicin, a closely related anthracycline, is a core component of the standard ABVD regimen for Hodgkin's lymphoma. Reviews in the compiled literature also list anthracyclines among the backbone agents for childhood leukaemia and Hodgkin lymphoma. The mechanistic rationale is therefore plausible.

However, the supporting trials are multi-agent regimens, so daunorubicin's own contribution cannot be separated. One trial involves paediatric non-Hodgkin lymphoma rather than Hodgkin lymphoma. Standard Hodgkin regimens use doxorubicin, not daunorubicin, so the prediction rests on class similarity rather than direct evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic link through anthracycline class is plausible, but there is no direct clinical evidence for daunorubicin in Hodgkin's lymphoma. The two Phase 2 trials are multi-drug regimens, one in non-Hodgkin lymphoma. Standard Hodgkin therapy already uses doxorubicin, and daunorubicin has no valid New Zealand licence. The package insert safety review is also incomplete.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Direct evidence for daunorubicin, or a justified rationale over doxorubicin, in Hodgkin's lymphoma
- Clarification of the cancelled licence status and the route to supply
- A cardiac and haematological monitoring plan, given the anthracycline toxicity profile
