## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not currently available. Pharmacology data show that tiotropium binds all five muscarinic receptor subtypes (M1–M5). Blocking these receptors relaxes airway smooth muscle and produces bronchodilation.

The high score most likely reflects the disease's closeness to other airway diseases in the model's network, not a specific mechanism. Bronchodilation could in theory help secondary airflow limitation in some people with a structural airway or lung malformation. No evidence shows that tiotropium treats the malformation itself or improves outcomes in this population.

## Safety Considerations

The data compiled for this report flag the following conditions as ones in which tiotropium may need caution, as is typical for anticholinergic drugs. They are not taken from package insert text:
- Angle-closure (closed-angle) glaucoma
- Benign prostatic hyperplasia
- Bladder neck obstruction
- Impaired renal function

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings, contraindications and drug interaction information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
No trial or publication directly studies tiotropium in respiratory malformation, so the evidence level is L5. The high score appears to reflect proximity to other airway diseases, not a mechanism specific to this condition. The available trials are in cystic fibrosis and cannot support efficacy here.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clinical or observational data in patients with respiratory malformation, particularly those with secondary airflow limitation
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the New Zealand products
- A defined target subpopulation, with a safety review covering the anticholinergic cautions above
