## One-Sentence Summary

Levonorgestrel is a synthetic progestin, available in New Zealand as tablets, intrauterine systems and a subdermal implant.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **Acne**, with a prediction score of **0.9988**.
Evidence is limited: **no registered clinical trials** and **1 randomized placebo-controlled trial** that tested levonorgestrel only in combination with ethinyl estradiol. The remaining literature is mostly review articles. The proposed mechanism is also doubtful.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Levonorgestrel is a progestin, a class of drugs that act on hormone pathways, and acne is a condition in which androgens play an important role in the oil-producing glands of the skin.

The mechanistic link is weak and may even point the wrong way:

- Benefit for acne from hormonal contraceptives is usually credited to the **estrogen component**, which raises sex hormone-binding globulin and lowers free testosterone. Anti-androgenic progestins such as chlormadinone acetate also contribute.
- Levonorgestrel is derived from testosterone and retains some **androgenic activity**. Reviews of progestin androgenicity suggest it could worsen acne in some users.
- Levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine systems and implants are not established acne treatments.

The very high prediction score is therefore not well supported by the mechanism. The evidence level of L2 rests on a single randomized trial of a combination pill. It does not show that levonorgestrel on its own treats acne.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only acne-specific randomized trial tested an ethinyl estradiol/levonorgestrel combination, so its result cannot be credited to levonorgestrel alone. Levonorgestrel's androgenic properties make the direction of effect uncertain. No clinical trials are registered, and the high prediction score is not backed by a clear mechanism.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data, and an analysis of whether levonorgestrel alone helps or worsens acne
- Full results of the randomized trial (PMID 12196750), including the placebo comparison and the contribution of the estrogen component
- Comparative evidence between levonorgestrel-containing and anti-androgenic progestin-containing regimens
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the New Zealand regulator
- Evidence on the approved dosage forms (tablets, intrauterine systems, implant) for any acne use
